### Question 1: Convertible Term Conversion
Read the question and select the best answer.
- It is the most appropriate form of protection for a debt obligation. (7%)
- It is the only form of Term insurance that builds cash value. (9%)
- It is the only form of Term insurance that pays dividends. (7%)
- It does not require the insured to prove insurability at conversion. (78%) [CORRECT]

AiLA Explanation:
In the context of life insurance policies, Convertible Term insurance is a type that allows policyholders to convert their term life insurance policy into a permanent policy, such as whole life insurance, without having to prove insurability at the time of conversion.
The correct statement about Convertible Term insurance is that it does not require the insured to prove insurability at conversion. This means that policyholders can switch to a permanent policy without undergoing a medical exam or providing additional evidence of their health status. This feature can be particularly beneficial for individuals whose health has deteriorated since they initially purchased their term policy.
The incorrect statements about Convertible Term insurance are as follows:
- It is not necessarily the most appropriate form of protection for a debt obligation, as this depends on the individual's needs and financial situation (decreasing term is typically used for debt obligations).
- It is not the only form of Term insurance that builds cash value; other forms, such as whole life insurance, also build cash value over time (term insurance does not build cash value).
- It is not the only form of Term insurance that pays dividends; participating policies, for example, may also pay dividends to policyholders.
Key Takeaway: Convertible Term insurance allows policyholders to convert their term life insurance policy into a permanent policy without having to prove insurability at the time of conversion, making it an attractive option for those with declining health.

### Question 2: Insurable Interest at Time of Application
Read the question and select the best answer.
- when the applicant and the insured are the same person (17%)
- when the beneficiary is a member of the applicant's immediate family (10%)
- when a third-party applicant names the insured's company as beneficiary (15%)
- when a third-party applicant names themselves beneficiary (58%) [CORRECT]

AiLA Explanation:
In the context of life insurance, insurable interest refers to the requirement that the applicant must be at risk of suffering a significant loss, either emotional or economic, if the insured person dies. This concept ensures that insurance policies are not taken out for malicious or speculative purposes.
In the given scenario, insurable interest must exist between the applicant and the insured at the time of application when a third-party applicant names themselves as the beneficiary. This is because, in this situation, the applicant is not the insured person, and their insurable interest needs to be established to ensure that they have a legitimate reason to receive the policy benefits upon the insured's death.
The other situations do not necessarily require the establishment of insurable interest at the time of application. When the applicant and insured are the same person, insurable interest is inherent, as the person is essentially insuring themselves. When the beneficiary is a member of the applicant's immediate family, it is generally assumed that there is a strong emotional or economic connection, and thus insurable interest exists. Similarly, when a third-party applicant names the insured's company as the beneficiary, insurable interest is often implied due to the economic relationship between the insured and their company.
Key Takeaway: Insurable interest must exist between the applicant and insured at the time of application when a third-party applicant names themselves as the beneficiary, as this ensures that the policy is taken out for legitimate reasons and not for malicious or speculative purposes.

### Question 3: Change of Occupation in Life Insurance
Read the question and select the best answer.
- The benefits are adjusted to reflect what the premium would purchase for this new occupation. (28%)
- The individual is covered for any cause of death except bull riding. (4%)
- It remains in effect as issued. (58%) [CORRECT]
- The premium is increased. (10%)

AiLA Explanation:
In this scenario, an individual purchases a life insurance policy while working as a librarian and later decides to become a bull rider in the rodeo. The question is about how this change in occupation affects their life insurance policy.
The correct statement about their life policy is that it remains in effect as issued. This is because life insurance policies are issued based on the facts and circumstances at the time of application. Changes in occupation, health, or other factors do not alter the coverage or conditions of the policy once it has been issued.
The incorrect answers suggest that the premium may be increased, the benefits may be adjusted, or that the individual is not covered for death caused by bull riding. These statements are incorrect because life insurance policies, unlike Disability Income policies, do not change due to any alterations in the policyholder's circumstances after the policy has been issued.
Key Takeaway: Life insurance policies are issued based on the facts at the time of application and remain in force as issued, regardless of changes in the policyholder's occupation, health, or other factors.

### Question 4: Viatical Settlements (Identify the False Statement)
Read the question and select the best answer.
- Viatical Settlements involve a third-party company. (10%)
- An Unconditional Right to Return (Free Look) is required with a Viatical Settlement. (15%)
- Payments received by a viator with a life expectancy of less than 5 years from the sale of a life policy to a viatical company are free from federal taxes. (62%) [CORRECT - STATEMENT IS FALSE]
- The viatical company becomes the beneficiary of the life policies it purchases. (13%)

AiLA Explanation & Notes:
A viatical settlement is a financial transaction in which a policyholder who is terminally or chronically ill (referred to as the "viator") sells their life insurance policy to a third-party company (a viatical settlement provider) for an immediate lump-sum cash payment.
Key characteristics of viatical settlements include:
- The Payout: The cash payout is typically less than the policy's full face death benefit, but significantly higher than its cash surrender value, usually ranging between 50% and 80% depending on the insured's life expectancy.
- Transfer of Responsibility: The viatical company takes over ownership of the policy, assumes responsibility for paying all future premiums, and collects the full death benefit when the insured passes away.
- Tax Treatment: Under federal tax law (such as IRC Section 101(g)), proceeds received by a terminally ill individual are generally exempt from federal income taxes if life expectancy is less than 24 months (2 years), NOT 5 years!
- Unconditional Right to Return: Viatical agreements require a rescission/free-look period.
