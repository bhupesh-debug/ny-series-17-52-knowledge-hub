# NY 17-52 High-Yield Practice Question Bank & Trap Analysis

## 1. Overview
This question bank provides authentic, exam-style practice questions covering the most commonly failed topics on the **NY Series 17-52 Life, Accident and Health** exam. Each question features full distractor rationale, trap breakdowns, and statutory references.

## 2. High-Yield Question Bank

### Question 1: Insurable Interest Timing
**Question**: Under New York law, when must an insurable interest exist between the policyowner and the insured for a life insurance contract to be legally valid?
- A) Only at the time of claim or death of the insured.
- B) Both at the inception of the policy and at the time of the claim.
- C) Only at the time the policy is applied for and issued.
- D) Continuously throughout the entire life of the contract.

**Correct Answer**: **C) Only at the time the policy is applied for and issued.**
- **Statutory Rationale**: Under general contract law and NYIL § 3205, insurable interest in life and health insurance is determined **only at inception**. The subsequent termination of the relationship (e.g. divorce, dissolution of business partnership) does not invalidate the policy or the beneficiary's right to proceeds.
- **Trap**: In property/casualty insurance, insurable interest must exist at the time of loss. Examiners try to trick candidates into applying the property insurance rule to life insurance.
- **Governing Concept**: [[insurable-interest-and-contract-elements]]

### Question 2: Health Insurance Reinstatement Waiting Period
**Question**: An insured's individual major medical health insurance policy lapses for nonpayment of premium. The insured submits a reinstatement application along with all unpaid premiums. The insurer accepts the premium without requiring an application, or issues a conditional receipt. When does coverage become effective for sickness?
- A) Immediately upon receipt of the premium.
- B) 10 days after the reinstatement date.
- C) 30 days after the reinstatement date.
- D) 45 days after the reinstatement date.

**Correct Answer**: **B) 10 days after the reinstatement date.**
- **Statutory Rationale**: Under the Mandatory Reinstatement provision (NYIL § 3216), accidental injuries are covered immediately upon reinstatement, but sickness is covered only after a **10-day probationary period**. This prevents adverse selection (i.e. people only paying back premiums after they wake up sick).
- **Trap**: Candidates often confuse the 45-day rule (the time the insurer has to reject an application before automatic reinstatement occurs) with the 10-day sickness coverage waiting period!
- **Governing Concept**: [[mandatory-and-optional-health-provisions]]

### Question 3: New York Regulation 60 Replacement Free Look
**Question**: When an existing whole life insurance policy is replaced with a new universal life policy in New York State, what is the mandatory minimum Free Look (Right to Examine) period that must be granted to the policyowner on the new policy?
- A) 10 days
- B) 20 days
- C) 30 days
- D) 60 days

**Correct Answer**: **C) 30 days**
- **Statutory Rationale**: While standard life insurance policies in New York require a 10-day free look period, **11 NYCRR Part 51 (Insurance Regulation 60)** explicitly mandates that any life insurance policy or annuity issued as a replacement MUST provide a **30-day Free Look period** with a 100% full refund of all premiums paid.
- **Trap**: Candidates who memorize only the standard 10-day free look get this question wrong every time.
- **Governing Concept**: [[new-york-insurance-regulation-60-replacement]]

### Question 4: Continuing Education in New York
**Question**: In order to renew a New York Life, Accident and Health Agent license, how many continuing education (CE) credit hours must the licensee complete during each two-year licensing cycle, and what is the minimum ethics requirement?
- A) 24 hours total, including 3 hours of ethics.
- B) 15 hours total, including 3 hours of ethics.
- C) 20 hours total, including 2 hours of ethics.
- D) 15 hours total, with no specific ethics mandate.

**Correct Answer**: **B) 15 hours total, including 3 hours of ethics.**
- **Statutory Rationale**: Under NYIL § 2132, resident licensees must complete **15 credit hours** of approved continuing education every two-year licensing period. At least **3 of these credit hours must be in courses devoted to insurance ethics and professionalism**.
- **Trap**: Many states require 24 hours of CE; New York specifically requires **15 hours**, making this a favorite state-specific trick question.
- **Governing Concept**: [[ny-specific-state-insurance-laws-mastery]]

### Question 5: Nonforfeiture Options Default
**Question**: A policyowner who has owned a whole life insurance policy for 12 years stops paying premiums and fails to select a nonforfeiture option within the 60-day statutory window. Under New York law, which nonforfeiture option will the insurance company automatically apply?
- A) Cash Surrender Value
- B) Reduced Paid-Up Insurance
- C) Extended Term Insurance
- D) Automatic Premium Loan

**Correct Answer**: **C) Extended Term Insurance**
- **Statutory Rationale**: Under standard nonforfeiture laws (NYIL § 4221), if the policyowner neglects to choose an option within the election period after default in premium payments, the insurer MUST automatically implement **Extended Term Insurance**, maintaining the full original face amount for as long as the net cash surrender value will purchase term insurance.
- **Governing Concept**: [[standard-life-policy-provisions-and-riders]]

### Question 6: Prohibited Sales Inducements (Rebating)
**Question**: An agent wishing to show appreciation to prospective clients distributes promotional gifts. Under New York Insurance Law § 2324 & 4224, what is the maximum retail value permitted for promotional items bearing the agent's or company's name before it is deemed illegal rebating?
- A) $10
- B) $25
- C) $50
- D) $100

**Correct Answer**: **B) $25**
- **Statutory Rationale**: New York Insurance Law permits agents and insurers to distribute promotional merchandise, calendars, or pens conspicuously printed with the insurer's or producer's name provided the retail value **does not exceed $25**. Anything exceeding $25 given in connection with insurance is unlawful rebating.
- **Governing Concept**: [[ny-specific-state-insurance-laws-mastery]]
