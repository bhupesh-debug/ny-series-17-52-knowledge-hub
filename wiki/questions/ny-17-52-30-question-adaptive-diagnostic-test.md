# NY Series 17-52 Accident & Health 30-Question Adaptive Diagnostic Exam

> **Diagnostic Notice**: This 30-question diagnostic exam is an instructional assessment designed to baseline candidate proficiency across the 5 core modules (6 questions per module). It does not represent the official 100-question exam weighting.
> 
> **Testing Protocol**: Complete Questions 1 through 30 in Section 1 without looking at the explanations. Once complete, proceed to Section 2 to score your responses, audit missed questions, and map remediation priorities.

## Detailed Item Explanations & Trap Audits

### Question 1: Application Alteration
- **Correct Option**: **B**
- **Reasoning**: Any modification to a formal insurance application must be authenticated by the applicant's initials or completed on a fresh form. Correction fluid or unauthorized producer scribbles are strictly prohibited.
- **Trap**: Distractor C is tempting because agents think they have power of attorney as field underwriters. They do not!

### Question 2: Representations (NYIL § 3105)
- **Correct Option**: **B**
- **Reasoning**: Under New York law, applicant statements are representations. An insurer can only rescind or void coverage during the first 2 years if a misrepresentation was material to the risk.
- **Trap**: Distractor A traps candidates who think insurance contracts require absolute warranties.

### Question 3: Incomplete Application
- **Correct Option**: **C**
- **Reasoning**: An incomplete application must be returned to the producer. If an insurer issues a policy without obtaining answers to blank questions, the insurer legally waives its defense.
- **Trap**: Distractor A suggests adding answers during the Free Look; this is illegal.

### Question 4: MIB Scope
- **Correct Option**: **C**
- **Reasoning**: The Medical Information Bureau contains coded reports of medical impairments. It does not contain credit reports, investigative character reports, or physical exams.
- **Trap**: Confusing MIB with Consumer Credit Reports or FCRA Inspection Reports.

### Question 5: Conditional Receipt Effective Date
- **Correct Option**: **B**
- **Reasoning**: Under a standard conditional receipt, coverage is retroactive to the date of the application or the date of the medical exam, WHICHEVER IS LATER (here, June 10), provided the applicant was insurable as applied for.
- **Trap**: Picking June 1 (application date) while forgetting that a required medical exam pushes the retroactive date to the exam completion date!

### Question 6: Delivery of Non-Prepaid Policy
- **Correct Option**: **B**
- **Reasoning**: If no premium was paid at application, coverage does not take effect until delivery. The producer must collect the initial premium and obtain a signed Statement of Continued Good Health.
- **Trap**: Distractor A allows free insurance during the Free Look without paying initial premium.

### Question 7: Morale Hazard
- **Correct Option**: **C**
- **Reasoning**: Morale hazard arises from carelessness, indifference, or laziness because insurance exists. Moral hazard involves conscious dishonesty or criminal fraud.
- **Trap**: Mixing up Moral (dishonest/bad intentions) vs. Morale (careless/indifferent).

### Question 8: Cost-Sharing Calculation
- **Correct Option**: **C**
- **Reasoning**:
  1. Total bill = $11,000.
  2. Insured pays deductible first: $1,000 (leaves $10,000).
  3. Coinsurance: Insured pays 20% of $10,000 = $2,000.
  4. Total paid by insured: $1,000 (deductible) + $2,000 (coinsurance) = **$3,000**. (Below the $5,000 stop-loss limit).
- **Trap**: Calculating 20% of the entire $11,000 without subtracting the deductible first ($2,200 + $1,000 = $3,200). Always subtract deductible first!

### Question 9: Coordination of Benefits (Birthday Rule)
- **Correct Option**: **B**
- **Reasoning**: Under the Birthday Rule, the parent whose birthday occurs earlier in the calendar year (March 22 vs. October 14) is primary. Chronological age (birth year 1985 vs 1988) is irrelevant!
- **Trap**: Picking Father because he is older by year. The rule looks ONLY at month and day!

### Question 10: Subrogation
- **Correct Option**: **B**
- **Reasoning**: Subrogation transfers the insured's recovery rights to the insurer, preventing the insured from double-dipping and holding the at-fault party responsible.
- **Trap**: Distractor A confuses subrogation with Coordination of Benefits.

### Question 11: Taxation of Group Disability
- **Correct Option**: **B**
- **Reasoning**: Because the employer paid 100% of the premium and deducted it as a business expense, 100% of the disability benefits received by the employee are taxable income.
- **Trap**: Confusing group disability with individual disability (where premiums are after-tax and benefits are tax-free).

### Question 12: ACA Pre-Existing Conditions
- **Correct Option**: **C**
- **Reasoning**: The ACA completely prohibits comprehensive individual and group medical plans from excluding pre-existing conditions or rating policies based on health status.
- **Trap**: Remembering the pre-ACA HIPAA 63-day gap rule (Distractor D), which no longer applies to major medical!

### Question 13: Monthly Grace Period
- **Correct Option**: **B**
- **Reasoning**: Under NYIL § 3216, grace periods are: 7 days for weekly premium, **10 days for monthly premium**, and 31 days for all other modes.
- **Trap**: Assuming all grace periods are 31 days. Monthly is 10 days!

### Question 14: Reinstatement Sickness Wait
- **Correct Option**: **B**
- **Reasoning**: Once reinstated, accidental injuries are covered immediately, but sickness is subject to a mandatory **10-day probationary waiting period** to prevent adverse selection.
- **Trap**: Confusing the 45-day automatic approval rule with the 10-day sickness waiting period!

### Question 15: Claims Sequence
- **Correct Option**: **B**
- **Reasoning**: Mandatory timelines: Notice of Claim within **20 days**; Proof of Loss within **90 days**.
- **Trap**: Confusing Notice of Claim (20 days) with Proof of Loss (90 days). Remember: **20-15-90-60-3**.

### Question 16: Legal Actions
- **Correct Option**: **B**
- **Reasoning**: The insured cannot file a lawsuit before **60 days** following proof of loss submission, and cannot sue after **3 years**.
- **Trap**: Picking 2 years (confusing Legal Actions with the 2-year Incontestable Clause).

### Question 17: Change of Occupation Provision
- **Correct Option**: **C**
- **Reasoning**: In disability income, switching to a more hazardous job causes the insurer to **reduce benefits** to what the premium paid would have purchased at the hazardous rate.
- **Trap**: In Life insurance, the policy remains as issued. In Disability, benefits are reduced!

### Question 18: Noncan vs. Guaranteed Renewable
- **Correct Option**: **B**
- **Reasoning**: Noncancelable guarantees that the insurer cannot cancel AND can NEVER raise premium rates. Guaranteed Renewable guarantees continuation but allows premium increases by class.
- **Trap**: Thinking Guaranteed Renewable guarantees frozen rates. It does not!

### Question 19: SSDI Definition & Waiting Period
- **Correct Option**: **C**
- **Reasoning**: SSDI requires total disability expected to last at least **12 continuous months or result in death**, with a mandatory **5-month waiting period** (benefits begin month 6).
- **Trap**: Confusing SSDI's 5-month wait with commercial disability elimination periods (e.g. 30, 90 days).

### Question 20: Medicare Part A Hospitalization
- **Correct Option**: **C**
- **Reasoning**: Medicare Part A covers up to **90 days** of inpatient hospital care per benefit period (days 1–60 fully paid after deductible; days 61–90 daily copay). 60 lifetime reserve days exist beyond day 90.
- **Trap**: Confusing the 60 full-pay days with the total 90-day benefit period.

### Question 21: MediGap / Medicare Advantage Ban
- **Correct Option**: **B**
- **Reasoning**: It is illegal to sell a MediGap policy to someone enrolled in Medicare Advantage (Part C) because MediGap only supplements Original Medicare (Parts A & B).
- **Trap**: Thinking MediGap can be used to pay Medicare Advantage copays. It cannot!

### Question 22: Workers' Comp vs. NY DBL
- **Correct Option**: **B**
- **Reasoning**: Workers' Comp covers on-the-job injuries with 100% medical care. NY DBL covers off-the-job sickness/injuries with **cash wage benefits only (max $170/wk, 26 wks)** and ZERO medical benefits!
- **Trap**: Believing NY DBL covers hospital bills. It never covers medical care!

### Question 23: NYS Partnership for LTC
- **Correct Option**: **B**
- **Reasoning**: NYS Partnership allows an insured who exhausts policy benefits to access New York Medicaid while retaining **100% of their assets (Total Asset Protection)**.
- **Trap**: Thinking the state refunds premiums or pays private indemnity forever. Medicaid pays, but assets are protected from recovery!

### Question 24: Medicaid Look-Back Period
- **Correct Option**: **D**
- **Reasoning**: The federal and New York Medicaid look-back period for institutional nursing home care is **60 months (5 years)**.
- **Trap**: Confusing 36 months (old rule) with the current 60-month statutory mandate.

### Question 25: HMO Characteristics
- **Correct Option**: **B**
- **Reasoning**: HMOs are closed networks requiring a Primary Care Physician gatekeeper for specialist visits and provide zero out-of-network coverage except emergency care.
- **Trap**: Thinking HMOs pay fee-for-service. Providers receive monthly capitation!

### Question 26: Disability Elimination Period
- **Correct Option**: **C**
- **Reasoning**: The elimination period is a deductible in time. Extending it (e.g. from 30 to 90 days) reduces the insurer's claims liability and significantly **lowers the premium**.
- **Trap**: Confusing the elimination period with the benefit period.

### Question 27: Hospital Indemnity Stated Dollar Payout
- **Correct Option**: **A**
- **Reasoning**: Hospital indemnity policies pay a fixed stated cash dollar benefit (e.g. $250/day x 5 days = $1,250) directly to the insured, regardless of actual hospital expenses or duplicate coverage.
- **Trap**: Attempting to apply coinsurance or medical expense reimbursement rules to an indemnity policy!

### Question 28: LTC Peril (Aging) & Respite Care
- **Correct Option**: **B**
- **Reasoning**: LTC protects against the chronic peril of aging and inability to perform ADLs. Respite care provides substitute care to give primary family caregivers a temporary break.
- **Trap**: Confusing respite care with convalescent care or hospice.

### Question 29: Presumptive Disability
- **Correct Option**: **B**
- **Reasoning**: Presumptive disability automatically qualifies the insured for total disability upon permanent loss of sight in both eyes, hearing, speech, or use of any two limbs, waiving elimination periods.
- **Trap**: Carpal tunnel or back pain require proving inability to work; they are not presumptive.

### Question 30: ACA Metal Tiers (Silver Plan)
- **Correct Option**: **B**
- **Reasoning**: ACA actuarial value tiers (BSGP): Bronze = 60%, **Silver = 70%**, Gold = 80%, Platinum = 90%.
- **Trap**: Inverting Bronze and Silver percentages. Remember the mnemonic **BSGP (60-70-80-90)**!
