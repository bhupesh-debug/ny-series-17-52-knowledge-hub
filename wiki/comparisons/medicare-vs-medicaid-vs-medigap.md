# Medicare vs. Medicaid vs. Medicare Supplement (MediGap)

## 1. Executive Summary
Senior health care coverage constitutes one of the heaviest testing concentrations in Division II (Accident & Health Insurance). Candidates are regularly tested on the operational boundaries, eligibility criteria, cost-sharing mechanics, and federal-state coordination between **Original Medicare (Parts A & B)**, **Medicare Advantage (Part C)**, **Prescription Drug Plans (Part D)**, **Medicare Supplement Insurance (MediGap)**, and **Medicaid**.

---

## 2. Comparison Matrix

| Dimension | Original Medicare (Title XVIII) | MediGap (Medicare Supplement) | Medicaid (Title XIX) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Administering Authority** | Federal (CMS / Social Security) | Private Insurers (Regulated by NYS DFS & NAIC) | State & Federal Joint (Administered by NYS Dept of Health) |
| **Primary Eligibility** | Age 65+, OR any age with End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) / ALS, OR receiving Social Security Disability for **24 months** | Must be enrolled in both **Medicare Part A & Part B** | Needs-based: Low income and low liquid assets |
| **Funding Source** | Payroll taxes (FICA) + Monthly Part B/D premiums + General federal revenues | 100% individual monthly premiums paid to private insurer | Federal grants matched with NY State and county tax revenues |
| **What it Covers** | Part A: Inpatient hospital, skilled nursing facility (SNF), hospice, home health.<br>Part B: Physician visits, outpatient, diagnostic tests, medical equipment. | Fills the "gaps" in Original Medicare (Part A/B deductibles, copayments, co-insurance, foreign travel emergency). | Comprehensive: Hospital, doctor, dental, vision, prescription drugs, and **Custodial Long-Term Nursing Home Care**. |
| **What it EXCLUDES** | Long-term custodial care, routine dental/vision/hearing, hearing aids, cosmetic surgery, private-duty nursing. | Does **NOT** cover long-term custodial nursing home care; cannot duplicate Medicare benefits. | Requires asset "spend-down"; subject to 60-month (5-year) look-back period for asset transfers in NY. |

## 3. Deep Dive: Medicare Architecture (Parts A, B, C, D)

- **Part A (Hospital Insurance)**:
  - Premium-free for individuals with 40 quarters (10 years) of Social Security work credits.
  - Inpatient hospital benefit period: Days 1–60 (deductible applies, $0 copay); Days 61–90 (daily copay); Days 91–150 (Lifetime Reserve Days, higher copay).
  - Skilled Nursing Facility (SNF): Requires prior **3-day inpatient hospital stay**; Days 1–20 ($0 copay); Days 21–100 (daily copay); Beyond day 100 ($0 Medicare coverage).
- **Part B (Medical Insurance)**:
  - Voluntary, requires monthly premium (deducted from Social Security check).
  - Annual deductible applies, then Medicare pays **80% of approved charges**, patient pays **20% co-insurance** (no annual out-of-pocket maximum!).
- **Part C (Medicare Advantage)**:
  - Managed care alternative (HMO, PPO) provided by private insurers approved by CMS.
  - Combines Parts A, B, and usually D.
  - *Trap*: A person CANNOT have both Medicare Advantage (Part C) and a MediGap policy simultaneously!
- **Part D (Prescription Drug Coverage)**:
  - Voluntary private prescription drug coverage. Monthly premium + deductible + copays/coinsurance.

## 4. MediGap (Medicare Supplement) in New York State

- **Standardized Plans**: Lettered **Plans A through N** (Plan A is core basic benefit; all insurers selling MediGap must offer Plan A).
- **New York Continuous Open Enrollment Rule**:
  - Unlike federal rules where MediGap open enrollment is limited to 6 months upon turning 65 and enrolling in Part B, **New York State requires continuous year-round open enrollment and community rating** for all MediGap policies! Insurers cannot deny coverage or charge higher rates based on age, gender, or health status.
- **Pre-existing Condition Waiting Period in NY**:
  - Maximum pre-existing condition exclusion period is **6 months**, which must be reduced or waived if the applicant had prior creditable coverage.
- **30-Day Free Look**:
  - All MediGap policies in New York must provide a mandatory **30-day Free Look** period with full refund of premium.

## 5. Exam Traps & Multiple Choice Distractors

> [!WARNING]
> **Trap 1: Long-Term Care & Nursing Homes**
> - Does Medicare or MediGap pay for custodial care in a nursing home?
> - **NO!** Medicare only covers medical/rehabilitative skilled nursing up to 100 days. Custodial care is paid out-of-pocket, by **Long-Term Care (LTC) Insurance**, or by **Medicaid** after spending down assets.
>
> **Trap 2: MediGap Plan A Mandatory Offering**
> - In New York, if an insurer offers any MediGap plan, they **MUST also offer Plan A (Core Basic Benefits)**.
>
> **Trap 3: Medicare Disability Eligibility**
> - An individual under age 65 qualifies for Medicare after receiving Social Security Disability Income (SSDI) benefits for **24 continuous months** (except ALS and ESRD, which qualify with little to no waiting period).
