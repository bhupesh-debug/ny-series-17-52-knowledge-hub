# Term Life vs. Whole Life vs. Universal Life Insurance

## 1. Executive Summary
Understanding the exact architectural distinctions between **Term Life**, **Ordinary Whole Life**, and **Universal Life (UL)** accounts for approximately 15–20% of the Life Insurance portion of the NY 17-52 exam. Test questions routinely test cash value growth, premium flexibility, death benefit options, and investment risk allocation.

---

## 2. Side-by-Side Comparison Matrix

| Dimension | Term Life | Traditional Whole Life (Permanent) | Universal Life (Flexible Permanent) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Coverage Period** | Temporary (e.g. 10, 20, 30 years or to age 65/70) | Permanent (to age 100 or 121) | Permanent (to age 100 or 121) |
| **Cash Value** | **NONE** (Pure protection) | Guaranteed cash value; grows based on fixed schedule | Unbundled cash value; credited with current interest (with guaranteed minimum floor) |
| **Premium Structure** | Level, Increasing, or Decreasing; non-flexible | **Fixed & Level** for life | **Flexible** (Can pay minimum, target, or maximum; can skip if cash value covers mortality) |
| **Death Benefit** | Fixed level, decreasing, or increasing | **Fixed & Level** (guaranteed) | **Flexible** (Option A: Level DB; Option B: Increasing DB = Face + Cash Value) |
| **Cost / Initial Premium** | Lowest initial cost per dollar of coverage | Higher initial cost than term | Moderate initial cost; flexible funding |
| **Policy Loans** | Not available | Available after cash value builds (typically 3 yrs) | Available; partial surrenders / withdrawals also permitted |
| **Separation of Elements** | Bundled (pure mortality cost) | Bundled (insurer manages investments, mortality, expenses) | **Unbundled** (Mortality charges, expense charges, and interest credit reported separately) |
| **Investment Risk** | None | Borne by **Insurer** (Guaranteed rates) | Borne primarily by **Insurer** (Subject to guaranteed minimum interest rate, e.g. 2–4%) |

## 3. Specialized Permanent Variations (High Exam Frequency)

- **Variable Life (VL)**:
  - Cash value invested in **Separate Accounts** (stocks, bonds, mutual funds).
  - Death benefit has a guaranteed minimum, but cash value is **NOT guaranteed**.
  - **Investment risk is borne entirely by Policyowner**.
  - Dual regulation: Requires both a **Life Insurance License** and a **FINRA Series 6 or 7 registration**, plus SEC oversight.
- **Variable Universal Life (VUL)**:
  - Combines flexible premiums of Universal Life with separate account investment choices of Variable Life. Dual regulation applies.
- **Indexed Universal Life (IUL)**:
  - Cash value growth is tied to an equity index (e.g., S&P 500), but has a **guaranteed floor (typically 0%)** and a **cap (typically 8–10%)**.
  - Regulated as a general life product, does not require FINRA registration.

## 4. Exam Traps & Multiple Choice Distractors

> [!WARNING]
> **Trap 1: Death Benefit Options in Universal Life**
> - **Option A (Option 1)**: Level death benefit. As cash value increases, pure insurance (net amount at risk) decreases. Cheaper.
> - **Option B (Option 2)**: Increasing death benefit. Death benefit = Face Amount + Current Cash Value. Mortality costs increase more rapidly as cash value grows.
>
> **Trap 2: Partial Surrender vs. Policy Loan**
> - Whole life allows **loans** (incurs interest, does not permanently reduce cash value if repaid).
> - Universal life allows both loans and **partial withdrawals/surrenders** (permanently reduces face amount and cash value, no interest charged).
>
> **Trap 3: Term Conversion Privilege**
> - Converting term to permanent life insurance requires **NO evidence of insurability** (no medical exam), provided it is done within the conversion window (e.g. within 10 years or prior to age 65).
> - Premiums on the new permanent policy are based on the insured's **attained age** at the time of conversion (or original age if backdated with retroactive premium).
