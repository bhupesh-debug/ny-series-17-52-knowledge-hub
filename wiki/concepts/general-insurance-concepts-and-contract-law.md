# General Insurance Concepts & Contract Law (Official 6% Weight)

## 1. Plain-English Concept (For a 10-Year-Old)
Insurance is a team safety net that transfers financial danger from you to an insurance company in exchange for a small fee called a premium.
- **Pure Risk vs. Speculative Risk**:
  - *Pure Risk*: Only two outcomes—either you get sick/hurt (loss), or you stay healthy (no loss). There is **no chance to win money**. Insurance *only* covers pure risk!
  - *Speculative Risk*: Like buying a lottery ticket or gambling at a casino—you might win or you might lose. Insurance **never** covers speculative risk!
- **Peril vs. Hazard**:
  - *Peril* is the actual punch that hurts you (fire, lightning, pneumonia, broken bone).
  - *Hazard* is the wet slippery banana peel on the floor that makes it much more likely you will slip and break a bone.
- **Contract Rules**:
  - *Adhesion*: The insurance company wrote every single word of the rulebook. If any sentence is confusing or has two meanings, **the court rules in favor of the customer**!
  - *Aleatory*: The trade is unequal. You might pay $100 in premium and get $50,000 in hospital bills paid, or pay premiums for 10 years and never file a claim.
  - *Unilateral*: Only **one party** makes a legally binding promise. The insurance company promises to pay your valid claims; you never promise to keep paying premiums (you can cancel anytime).

## 2. Risk Management Strategies (Mnemonic: "ARRTS")
1. **Avoidance**: Completely eliminating the risk exposure (e.g., quitting a dangerous job, never riding a motorcycle).
2. **Reduction**: Lowering the chance or severity of a loss (e.g., installing smoke alarms, wearing a seatbelt, annual wellness exams).
3. **Retention**: Keeping the risk yourself and paying out of pocket (e.g., deductibles, self-insurance).
4. **Transfer**: Passing the financial risk to someone else (e.g., **buying an insurance policy**).
5. **Sharing**: Distributing risk among a group with similar exposures.

## 3. Legal Elements & Contract Principles
- **Elements of Legal Contract (ACCL)**: Agreement (Offer & Acceptance), Consideration (Premium + application statements vs. promise to pay), Competent Parties, Legal Purpose.
- **Warranties vs. Representations**:
  - *Representation*: A statement believed to be true to the best of knowledge and belief. Application answers are deemed representations.
  - *Warranty*: A statement guaranteed to be literally true. A breach voids the contract.
- **Waiver & Estoppel**:
  - *Waiver*: Voluntarily giving up a known legal right (e.g., insurer accepts late premiums repeatedly without question).
  - *Estoppel*: Legal doctrine preventing the insurer from re-asserting that right later after the customer relied on their conduct.

## 5. Tricky Exam Scenario & Trap Analysis
> **Question**: A person resigns from a high-risk commercial fishing job to eliminate the chance of an occupational injury at sea. Which risk management technique does this illustrate?
> - A) Retention
> - B) Avoidance
> - C) Sharing
> - D) Transfer
>
> **Correct Answer**: **B) Avoidance**
> - **Explanation**: The individual completely eliminated the exposure to loss rather than insuring it (transfer), mitigating it (reduction), or paying for it out-of-pocket (retention).
> - **Tempting Wrong Answer**: **D (Transfer)** is tempting if candidates assume every risk question relates to buying an insurance policy.
