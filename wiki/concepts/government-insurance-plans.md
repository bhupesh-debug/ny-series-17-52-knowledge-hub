# Government Insurance Plans (Official 9% Weight)

## 1. Plain-English Concept (For a 10-Year-Old)
Government programs are public safety nets created by law for specific groups:
- **Workers' Compensation**: For injuries that happen **ON the job** at work. Pays 100% of doctor/hospital bills and replaces part of your wages.
- **New York DBL**: For sickness or injuries that happen **OFF the job** (like breaking a leg on a weekend trip). Pays a small weekly cash check, but **$0 for hospital bills**.
- **Social Security Disability (SSDI)**: Severe federal program. You must have a disability so terrible that you cannot do any work at all, expected to last at least **12 months** or result in death, with a strict **5-month waiting period**.
- **Medicare**: Federal health plan for folks age **65 and older**, or younger people with long-term disabilities (after 24 months of SSDI) or kidney failure (ESRD).
- **Medicaid**: State and federal joint program for people with **limited income and low financial assets**.

---

## 2. Core Program Rules & Distinctions
- **Medicare Structure**:
  - *Part A (Hospital)*: Inpatient hospital stays, skilled nursing facility (SNF under defined conditions), hospice, home health.
  - *Part B (Medical)*: Physician visits, outpatient hospital services, durable medical equipment. (Requires voluntary monthly premium; 80/20 coinsurance).
  - *Part C (Medicare Advantage)*: Private managed care alternative to Original Medicare.
  - *Part D (Prescriptions)*: Standalone prescription drug coverage through private approved plans.
  - *CRITICAL RULE*: **Medicare DOES NOT cover ongoing custodial long-term nursing care!**
- **Medicaid (Title XIX)**:
  - Jointly funded by federal and state governments; administered in NY by the Department of Health.
  - Means-tested (income and asset criteria apply). Covers hospital, doctor, prescription drugs, and **custodial nursing home care**. Subject to 60-month look-back on asset transfers.
- **Child Health Plus (New York)**:
  - New York's state health insurance plan for children under age 19; sliding scale premiums based on family income.
- **Workers' Comp vs. New York DBL (CRITICAL EXAM NUMBERS)**:
  - *Workers' Comp*: Work-related; pays medical bills + disability income.
  - *NY DBL (Statutory Baseline)*: Non-work related; **50% of average weekly wage up to statutory cap of $170/week**, for up to **26 weeks**, with a **7-day waiting period** (begins on 8th day). Cash wage replacement only; no medical bill coverage!

---

## 3. Recall Cue
- **A = Admission (Hospital); B = Both doctors and outpatient; C = Choice of private plan; D = Drugs.**
- **NY DBL = Non-work, $170 cap, 26 weeks, 7-day wait, Cash only.**
- **Medicare ≠ Ongoing Custodial Nursing Care.**

---

## 4. Tricky Exam Scenario & Trap Analysis
> **Question**: Which healthcare program is designed primarily for eligible individuals with limited income and assets and is jointly funded by federal and state governments?
> - A) Medicare
> - B) Medicaid
> - C) Medigap
> - D) Medicare Advantage
>
> **Correct Answer**: **B) Medicaid**
> - **Explanation**: Medicaid is the joint federal-state means-tested program administered by states under federal guidelines. Medicare is a purely federal social insurance program based on age or disability, not income.
> - **Tempting Wrong Answer**: **A (Medicare)** is tempting if candidates mix up Medicare (age/disability) and Medicaid (income/needs).
