# Disability Income Insurance: Definitions, Riders & Business Uses

## 1. Plain-English Concept (For a 10-Year-Old)
Disability insurance is **"Paycheck Insurance."** If you get hurt or sick and cannot work, it pays you a monthly allowance so you can buy groceries and pay rent.
- **The Elimination Period is the "Time Deductible"**: If you have a 30-day elimination period, you must be home sick with no paycheck for 30 full days before the insurance company starts paying you.
- **The Benefit Period is the "Time Clock"**: How long the checks will keep arriving (e.g., 2 years, 5 years, or until age 65).
- **Own Occupation vs. Any Occupation**:
  - **Own Occ (Best / Friendly)**: A surgeon cuts her hand. She cannot do surgery anymore, but she can still teach medical school classes. Under "Own Occ," she gets her **full disability check** because she cannot do her *own* specific job!
  - **Any Occ (Tough / Strict)**: The insurance company tells the surgeon: "You can't operate, but you can sit in a booth at a parking lot or teach. Since you can do *any* job suited to your education, we pay you **nothing**!"

## 2. Core Definitions & Provisions

| Provision / Term | Exam Definition | Critical Detail |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Own Occupation** | Inability to perform the material and substantial duties of the insured's **regular, specific occupation**. | Most favorable to insured; highest premium; often switches to "Any Occ" after 2 years. |
| **Any Occupation** | Inability to perform the duties of **any occupation** for which the insured is reasonably suited by education, training, or experience. | Stricter; harder to qualify; lower premium. |
| **Presumptive Disability** | Automatic qualification for total disability benefits without requiring ongoing medical exams or proof of loss of earnings. | Triggered by: (1) Total blindness, (2) Total deafness, (3) Loss of speech, (4) Loss of two limbs. |
| **Elimination Period** | Waiting period (time deductible) between onset of disability and when benefits begin to accrue. | Longer elimination period = **Lower premium** (inversely related). |
| **Benefit Period** | Maximum duration benefits will be paid for a continuous disability. | Longer benefit period = **Higher premium**. |
| **Recurrent Disability** | When a prior disability reoccurs within a specified timeframe (typically **6 months**). | Treated as continuation of prior claim; insured **does NOT have to satisfy a new elimination period**! |

## 3. High-Yield Disability Riders

1. **Cost of Living Adjustment (COLA) Rider**: Automatically increases monthly disability benefit annually during an active claim to keep pace with inflation (CPI).
2. **Social Insurance Supplement (SIS) Rider**: Pays additional monthly benefits if government programs (Social Security Disability or Workers' Comp) deny benefits or pay less than expected.
3. **Guaranteed Insurability Rider (Future Increase Option)**: Allows insured to purchase additional monthly disability coverage at specified future ages or income milestones **WITHOUT medical underwriting / evidence of insurability**.
4. **Waiver of Premium**: Waives future disability insurance premiums after the insured has been totally disabled for a qualifying period (typically **90 days to 6 months**), and refunds premiums paid during the waiting period.

## 4. Business Disability Uses & Taxation

```text
================================================================================
BUSINESS DISABILITY SUMMARY TABLE
================================================================================
Policy Type             Who Pays?        Tax Deductible?   Benefit Taxable?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Individual Disability   Insured (After-tax)    NO               NO (100% Tax-Free)
Group Disability        Employer (Pre-tax)     YES (Biz Expense) YES (Taxable Income)
Business Overhead (BOE) Employer/Biz           YES (Biz Expense) YES (Taxable Income)*
Key Person Disability   Employer/Biz           NO               NO (Tax-Free)
Disability Buy-Out      Partners/Biz           NO               NO (Tax-Free)
================================================================================
*Note on BOE: Although the BOE benefit check is taxable income, the actual overhead
expenses paid (rent, utilities, employee salaries) are tax-deductible expenses!
```

## 5. Exam Trap Question & Distractor Analysis

> **Scenario**: A business owner purchases a Business Overhead Expense (BOE) disability insurance policy. If the owner becomes disabled, which of the following expenses will the policy **NEVER** reimburse?
> - A) Office rent and utility bills.
> - B) The salary of the disabled business owner.
> - C) Salaries of non-owner administrative employees.
> - D) Property taxes on the business premises.
>
> **Correct Answer**: **B) The salary of the disabled business owner.**
> - **Rationale**: BOE policies cover ongoing operating expenses (rent, utilities, employee payroll, accounting fees) to keep the business open while the owner is disabled. It **never pays the disabled owner's salary** (the owner needs an *Individual Disability Income* policy for their own lost compensation!).
> - **Tempting Wrong Answer**: **C** is tempting because people think all salaries are excluded. Administrative staff salaries ARE covered; only the owner's compensation is excluded!
