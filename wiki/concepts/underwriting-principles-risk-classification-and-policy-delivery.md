# Underwriting Principles, Risk Classification & Policy Delivery

## 1. Executive Summary
Underwriting is the risk selection and classification process through which an insurance company evaluates applicants to determine acceptability, terms of coverage, and appropriate premium rates. The underwriting cycle begins the instant a completed application and initial premium are received, continues through information gathering and actuarial pricing, and concludes upon successful policy delivery by the producer.

---

## 2. Information Sources in Underwriting

The underwriter relies on multiple sources of evidence to evaluate an applicant's potential risk:

| Information Source | Primary Function & Scope | Statutory & Regulatory Guardrails | Exam Trap / Tested Nuance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The Application** | **Primary source** of underwriting information. Part 1 (General Information), Part 2 (Medical History), Part 3 (Producer's Report). | NYIL § 3105 deems all applicant statements to be **representations**, not warranties. | The application is always the starting point. An incomplete application **cannot be accepted with info added later**—it must be returned to the producer. |
| **Producer's Report (Agent's Statement)** | Field observations made by the producer regarding applicant's character, financial status, and observed health. | Completed by agent; does NOT become part of the entire contract delivered to the insured. | Often confused with the Inspection Report. The Producer's report is filled by the agent, not an outside investigative agency. |
| **Attending Physician's Statement (APS)** | Detailed clinical history requested from the applicant's personal treating physician. | Underwriter requests this when the application or MIB reveals questionable health conditions. | Only ordered when **specific questionable medical concerns** require verification; not ordered on every routine application. |
| **Medical Information Bureau (MIB)** | Non-profit centralized database of confidential **medical impairment codes** shared among member insurers. | Governed by Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA); requires written applicant authorization; applicant has right to know if adverse action was influenced by MIB. | **MIB does NOT contain credit reports, investigative character reports, or physician examination reports.** An insurer CANNOT reject an applicant solely based on MIB data—they must independently verify. |
| **Inspection Report (Investigative Consumer Report)** | Independent third-party report investigating applicant's financial stability, moral character, hobbies, and lifestyle through interviews. | Governed by **Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA)**. Insurer must provide applicant with written Notice of Information Practices within 3 days. | Used for personal habits and moral risk. Does not provide clinical medical diagnosis. |
| **Medical Examination & Paramedical** | Physical exam (blood pressure, blood, urine, EKG, height/weight) conducted by a physician or paramedical technician. | Paid for entirely by the insurer. Required for high face amounts or questionable medical history. | Required for high amounts or older applicants; not needed for simplified issue term policies. |

## 3. Premium Calculation & Actuarial Foundations

Insurance premiums are derived from actuarial science based on three core components:

$$\mathbf{Gross\ Premium} = \mathbf{Risk\ (Mortality\ or\ Morbidity)} + \mathbf{Operating\ Expenses\ (Loading)} - \mathbf{Interest\ Earned}$$

$$\mathbf{Net\ Premium} = \mathbf{Risk\ (Mortality\ or\ Morbidity)} - \mathbf{Interest\ Earned}$$

### The Three Drivers of Premium
1. **Risk (Mortality or Morbidity)**:
   - **Mortality Tables**: Measure the probability of death at each age and gender. Used in **Life Insurance**.
   - **Morbidity Tables**: Measure the probability and duration of sickness, illness, or disability in a population. Used in **Health & Disability Insurance**.
2. **Operating Expenses (Loading)**:
   - Insurer overhead: producer commissions, administrative salaries, medical exam costs, regulatory compliance, and contingency reserves.
3. **Interest**:
   - Insurers invest premium dollars into income-producing assets (bonds, mortgages). The anticipated investment yield is **subtracted** from the cost, reducing the premium the insured must pay.

> [!TIP]
> **Formula Memory Hook**: Premium is what you pay: **Risk + Expense - Interest**. (Mortality/Morbidity and Expenses increase premium; Investment Interest decreases premium).

## 4. Risk Classifications & Underwriting Outcomes

After compiling all underwriting data, applicants are categorized into four standard risk tiers:

```
[Preferred Risk]      -> Lowest Risk / Clean Lifestyle   -> Discounted Premium
[Standard Risk]       -> Average Risk / Normal Mortality  -> Standard Premium
[Substandard (Rated)] -> Higher Risk / Impairments        -> Higher Premium ("Rated Policy")
[Declined]            -> Uninsurable Risk Profile         -> Coverage Rejected
```

### Methods of Rating Substandard Policies
When an applicant is substandard (e.g., uncontrolled hypertension, hazardous hobby, diabetic history), the insurer issues a **rated policy**:
- **Rated-up Age**: Premium is calculated as if the applicant were 5 or 10 years older.
- **Flat Extra Premium**: A fixed dollar amount added per $1,000 of face amount (e.g., $5 extra per $1,000 for aviation or scuba diving).
- **Tabular / Percentage Rating**: Premium is increased by a percentage of the standard rate (e.g., 150%, 200%, 300% table rating).
- **Lien or Limitation (Rare in modern life; common in health)**: Reducing benefit payout during early policy years or adding exclusionary impairment riders.

## 5. Policy Delivery & Field Underwriting Duties

The producer's responsibilities during policy delivery differ depending on whether the initial application was **prepaid** or **non-prepaid**:

```
                       Application Submitted
                                 |
        +------------------------+------------------------+
        |                                                 |
[Application Prepaid]                            [Application NOT Prepaid]
(Conditional Receipt Issued)                      (No Premium Paid with App)
        |                                                 |
Underwriting Approved                             Underwriting Approved
        |                                                 |
Physical Delivery to Client                       Physical Delivery to Client
  - Review benefits, riders, exclusions             - Collect full initial premium
  - Free Look starts on DELIVERY date               - Obtain signed Statement of Good Health
  - NO good health statement required               - Free Look starts on DELIVERY date
  - NO additional premium required                  - Coverage becomes effective upon collection
```

### Critical Policy Delivery Rules for the Exam:
1. **Free-Look (Right to Examine) Countdown**:
   - The 10-day (standard) or **30-day (NY Regulation 60 Replacement)** Free Look clock begins on the **DATE OF PHYSICAL DELIVERY** to the policyowner, NEVER on the policy date, application date, or issue date.
2. **Statement of Continued Good Health**:
   - Only required if premium was **NOT paid at the time of application**.
   - Verifies that the applicant's health has not deteriorated between the application date and the policy delivery date.
3. **Explaining Policy Provisions**:
   - The agent's primary duty at delivery is to review policy benefits, exclusions, effective dates, and attached riders with the client to prevent misunderstandings and buyer's remorse.

## 6. High-Yield Policy Mechanics & Statutory Rules

### A. Change of Occupation: Life vs. Disability Income
- **In Life Insurance**: The policy remains **IN EFFECT AS ISSUED** regardless of future occupational changes. If a librarian later becomes a rodeo bull rider or stunt pilot, the insurer cannot raise the premium, reduce the death benefit, or cancel the policy.
- **In Disability Income / Health Insurance**: Under the optional *Change of Occupation* provision (NYIL § 3216), if the insured switches to a more hazardous occupation, the insurer **reduces the disability benefit** to what the premium would have purchased at the hazardous rate. If switching to a less hazardous job, premium is reduced and excess unearned premium refunded.

### B. Convertible Term Insurance
- Allows the policyowner to convert a term policy into a permanent cash value policy (e.g., whole life) **WITHOUT PROVING INSURABILITY**.
- The conversion premium can be based on either **Attained Age** (current age at conversion, higher premium) or **Original Age** (age when term policy was first issued, requires retroactive lump-sum catch-up payment).
- Decreasing term is best suited for debt obligations (mortgages), while level convertible term provides flexible permanent conversion.

### C. Viatical Settlements (Taxation & Regulations)
- A financial transaction where a terminally or chronically ill insured (**viator**) sells an existing life policy to a third-party company (**viatical settlement provider**) for a discounted lump-sum payment (typically 50%–80% of face value).
- **Federal Tax Exemption (IRC § 101(g))**: Proceeds received by a terminally ill viator are completely **exempt from federal income taxes** ONLY IF certified by a physician as having a life expectancy of **less than 24 months (2 years)**. (Examiners love the distractor: *"life expectancy less than 5 years"*).
- **Rights & Ownership**: The viatical provider becomes the absolute owner and irrevocable beneficiary, assumes responsibility for all future premiums, and collects the entire death benefit when the viator passes away.
- **Cooling-Off / Free Look**: In New York (NYIL Article 78), viatical settlements mandate an unconditional right to rescind within 15 calendar days of receipt of proceeds.

### D. Long-Term Care (LTC) Peril & Respite Care
- **Core Peril**: LTC covers the peril of **aging** and chronic physical or cognitive decline, rather than acute accidental trauma or short-term medical sickness.
- **Respite Care**: A core LTC benefit that pays for a substitute caregiver to provide **temporary relief to primary family/informal caregivers**.

### E. Ambulatory Care & Medical Expense Exclusions
- **Ambulatory Care**: Medical diagnosis and treatment administered on an **outpatient basis** without overnight hospitalization.
- **Medical Expense Policy Coverage**: Covers prescription drugs, hospital stays, surgical fees, physician visits, HIV-AIDS medical treatments, and accidental bodily injuries.
- **Exclusion**: **Pain and suffering** is a legal/tort damage awarded in liability lawsuits, NOT a medical expense benefit under health insurance contracts.

## 7. Exam Traps & Multiple Choice Distractors

> [!WARNING]
> **Trap 1: Viatical Settlement Tax-Free Timeframe**
> - Distractor: "Tax-free if life expectancy is under 5 years." -> **WRONG!** Must be **less than 2 years (24 months)** under IRC § 101(g).
>
> **Trap 2: Incomplete Application Shortcut**
> - Distractor: "The underwriter can accept the incomplete application and add the answers later." -> **WRONG!** Incomplete applications **must be returned to the producer**. If the company issues a policy anyway, they waive the right to that information!
>
> **Trap 3: Change of Occupation in Life Insurance**
> - Distractor: "If an insured becomes a bull rider, the life insurer raises the premium or adjusts benefits." -> **WRONG!** In Life Insurance, it remains **in effect as issued**. (Adjusting benefits occurs in Disability Income policies).
>
> **Trap 4: MIB Report Contents**
> - Distractor: "The MIB provides credit reports and moral character interviews." -> **WRONG!** MIB contains **only coded medical impairment information**.
>
> **Trap 5: Free-Look Effective Date**
> - Distractor: "The 10-day free look starts on the policy issue date." -> **WRONG!** The countdown begins on the **date the policy is physically delivered** to the owner.
