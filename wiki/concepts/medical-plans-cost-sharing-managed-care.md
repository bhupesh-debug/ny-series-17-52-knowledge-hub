# Medical Plans, Cost Sharing & Managed Care

## 1. Plain-English Concept (For a 10-Year-Old)
Imagine a playground with different rules for seeing doctors:
- **Traditional Indemnity (Fee-for-Service)**: You can visit ANY doctor you want in the whole city. You pay the doctor cash, bring the receipt back to your insurance company, and they pay you back a big part of it.
- **HMO (Health Maintenance Organization)**: You get a "Team Captain" doctor called a **Primary Care Physician (PCP) / Gatekeeper**. If your ear hurts, you CANNOT just go see an ear specialist. You MUST ask your PCP first for a permission slip (called a **referral**). If you go outside the HMO playground (except for life-or-death emergencies), the HMO pays **zero dollars**!
- **PPO (Preferred Provider Organization)**: You have a club list of "favorite" in-network doctors. You can go outside the club list, but you have to pay much more out of your own piggy bank. Good news: **NO gatekeeper PCP and NO referrals needed**!
- **EPO (Exclusive Provider Organization)**: Like an HMO because there is **NO out-of-network coverage**, but like a PPO because you **don't need a gatekeeper referral** to see an in-network specialist.
- **POS (Point of Service)**: A hybrid "pick-at-the-moment-you-need-care" plan. You have an HMO gatekeeper, but at the "point of service," you can choose to go out of network if you are willing to pay higher deductibles and coinsurance.

## 2. Cost-Sharing Mechanics (Exam Rules & Calculations)

```text
Total Medical Bill
  │
  ├─► [1] Insured pays DEDUCTIBLE first out-of-pocket
  │
  ├─► [2] Remaining balance split: COINSURANCE (e.g., 80% Insurer / 20% Insured)
  │
  └─► [3] Once Insured's total payments hit STOP-LOSS (Out-of-Pocket Maximum):
            Insurer pays 100% of all covered expenses for the rest of the year!
```

- **Deductible**: Stated dollar amount the insured must pay before policy benefits begin.
- **Copayment (Copay)**: Fixed flat dollar fee (e.g., $25 per office visit) paid at the time of service.
- **Coinsurance**: Percentage sharing of expenses after the deductible (commonly 80/20 or 70/30).
- **Stop-Loss Provision (Out-of-Pocket Limit)**: The maximum dollar cap an insured pays in deductible + coinsurance during a calendar year. Once reached, insurer pays **100%**.

## 3. High-Yield Managed Care Comparison Matrix

| Feature | Indemnity | HMO | PPO | EPO | POS |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Provider Network** | Any provider | Strictly In-Network | In & Out-of-Network | Strictly In-Network | In & Out-of-Network |
| **Primary Care Physician (PCP)** | None | **Mandatory Gatekeeper** | None | None | **Mandatory Gatekeeper** |
| **Specialist Referral** | Not needed | **Mandatory** | Not needed | Not needed | **Mandatory** |
| **Out-of-Network Coverage** | Yes (Anywhere) | **NO** (Emergency only) | Yes (Higher cost) | **NO** (Emergency only) | Yes (Higher cost) |
| **Payment Mechanism** | Fee-for-Service reimbursement | **Capitation** (Fixed monthly fee per head) | Negotiated Discounted Fee-for-Service | Negotiated Discounted Fee-for-Service | Mixed (Capitation & Fee) |

## 4. Limited Policies (High Frequency Definitions)
- **Hospital Indemnity (Hospital Confinement / Fixed Rate)**: Pays a flat daily cash benefit (e.g., $100/day) directly to the insured for each day hospitalized, regardless of actual medical bills.
- **Dread Disease (Critical Illness / Specified Disease)**: Covers only one specifically named disease (e.g., Cancer, Heart Attack).
- **Accidental Death & Dismemberment (AD&D)**: Principal sum (capital amount) paid for death or double dismemberment (loss of 2 limbs or total sight); Capital sum (50%) paid for single dismemberment (loss of 1 limb or 1 eye).

## 5. Memory Hooks & Mnemonics
- **HMO = Home/Mini playground, Must have gatekeeper, Outside = Zero.**
- **PPO = Preferred Provider, Pick any specialist, Pay more Out-of-network.**
- **EPO = Exclusive Playground Only (No Out-of-network, but No gatekeeper).**
- **AD&D sums = Principal for Passing away (Death); Capital for Chopping off a limb.**

## 6. Exam Trap Question & Distractor Analysis

> **Scenario**: An insured covered under an HMO wakes up with knee pain. Wanting immediate expert advice, the insured goes directly to an in-network orthopedic surgeon without visiting their Primary Care Physician (PCP). The HMO denies the claim. Which provision justifies the denial?
> - A) The coinsurance requirement was not satisfied.
> - B) The gatekeeper / referral system was bypassed.
> - C) The specialist was out-of-network.
> - D) Orthopedic care is an excluded benefit under HMO law.
>
> **Correct Answer**: **B) The gatekeeper / referral system was bypassed.**
> - **Rationale**: In an HMO, members MUST obtain a referral from their primary care physician (gatekeeper) before seeing a specialist, even if the specialist is in-network.
> - **Tempting Wrong Answer**: **C** is tempting because people think claims are only denied when out-of-network. In HMOs, in-network specialist claims are denied if the gatekeeper is skipped!
