# New York Insurance Regulation 187: Suitability & Best Interest

## 1. Executive Summary
Promulgated by the **[[new-york-state-department-of-financial-services|NYS Department of Financial Services (DFS)]]** under **11 NYCRR Part 224**, **Insurance Regulation 187** establishes a groundbreaking regulatory framework requiring producers (agents and brokers) and insurers to act in the **BEST INTEREST** of the consumer when making recommendations regarding **life insurance policies and annuity contracts**.

Under Regulation 187, a producer cannot merely recommend a product because it is "suitable"—the recommendation must be based solely on the consumer's insurance needs and financial objectives, **WITHOUT REGARD to the financial or other interests of the producer or insurer**.

---

## 2. Core Pillars of the "Best Interest" Standard

1. **Only in the Consumer's Interest**:
   - The transaction recommended must address the consumer's identified financial needs, objectives, and circumstances at the time of the recommendation.
   - The producer must believe that the consumer will benefit from one or more core features of the policy (e.g., death benefit, income stream, tax deferral).
2. **Exclusion of Producer's Compensation**:
   - A producer’s compensation, receipt of incentives, commission differences between products, or sales quotas **cannot influence the recommendation in any manner**.
3. **Suitability Information Required (Consumer Profile)**:
   Producers must gather and document comprehensive consumer suitability information before recommending a policy:
   - Age, annual income, net worth, and liquid net worth
   - Financial situation and needs, including tax status and assets
   - Financial experience and investment/risk objectives
   - Intended use of the policy or annuity
   - Financial time horizon and existing assets
   - Liquidity needs and liquid net worth
   - Risk tolerance and willingness to accept non-guaranteed elements

---

## 3. Producer Duties & Documentation Mandates
- **Written Disclosure**: The producer must disclose to the consumer in writing the basis for the recommendation, including why the product meets their specific objectives and how it compares to reasonable alternatives.
- **Record Retention**: Insurers and producers must retain all suitability information, disclosures, and supervisory records for **at least 6 years** after the transaction is completed.
- **Prohibition on Misleading Self-Titles**: Producers cannot present themselves as financial planners, wealth advisers, or consultants unless they are properly licensed as such and actively provide comprehensive planning services beyond insurance sales.

---

## 4. Insurer Supervision Responsibilities
Under Regulation 187, insurers are directly responsible for establishing and maintaining a supervisory system reasonably designed to achieve compliance:
- Must establish written procedures and conduct periodic audits.
- Must provide comprehensive product training to producers before permitting them to solicit life insurance or annuities.
- Must conduct an independent suitability review of every recommended transaction **prior to issuing** the policy or contract.

---

## 5. Scope: Life Insurance vs. Annuity Applicability
- **Annuities**: Applies to all recommendations concerning sales, exchanges, surrenders, and in-force transactions.
- **Life Insurance**: Applies to all term, whole life, universal life, and variable life recommendations.
- **Exemptions**:
  - Direct response solicitations where no recommendation is made by a producer.
  - Group life or annuities where the certificate holder does not pay premiums or make individual investment decisions.
  - Credit life and credit disability insurance.

---

## 6. Exam Traps & Pitfalls (HIGH YIELD)

> [!WARNING]
> **Trap 1: "Best Interest" vs. Traditional "Suitability"**
> - Old standard (Suitability): Product fits generally, even if another product pays higher commission to the agent.
> - **New York Reg 187 Standard (Best Interest)**: Producer must act with prudence, care, and diligence, putting the consumer's interest **exclusively first**. Commission differences CANNOT influence the recommendation.
>
> **Trap 2: Record Retention Duration**
> - How long must Reg 187 suitability records be maintained in New York?
> - Answer: **At least 6 years**.
>
> **Trap 3: In-Force Transactions**
> - Does Reg 187 only apply to new sales?
> - **NO!** It applies to recommendations regarding existing in-force policies (e.g., surrendering, altering premium modes, executing loans, exercising nonforfeiture options).
