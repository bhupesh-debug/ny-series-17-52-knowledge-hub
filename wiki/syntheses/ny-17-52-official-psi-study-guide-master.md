## 2. Topic-by-Topic Breakdown (All 11 Official Sections)

### Section 1: Medical Plans (15% Weight)
- **Plain English & Example**: Medical insurance pays for eligible healthcare costs. Traditional indemnity lets you visit any doctor and reimburses covered expenses. Managed care uses provider networks and cost rules: an HMO requires a Primary Care Physician (PCP) gatekeeper to coordinate care and write specialist referrals.
- **Rules Tested**:
  - *Fee-for-service / Indemnity*: Provider bills for each service; plan reimburses based on reasonable charges and policy terms.
  - *Benefit Schedule*: Pays a stated flat dollar amount for a service regardless of actual billed costs.
  - *Major Medical*: Comprehensive coverage with deductible, coinsurance, and out-of-pocket stop-loss.
  - *HMO*: In-network only; mandatory PCP gatekeeper and referrals; emergency care covered out-of-network.
  - *PPO*: Network discounts; out-of-network care covered at higher cost-sharing; no gatekeeper or referrals needed.
  - *EPO*: Exclusive in-network care only (except emergencies); no out-of-network benefits; no gatekeeper needed.
  - *POS*: Combines HMO gatekeeper with out-of-network reimbursement at higher cost.
  - *Utilization Review*: Prospective (before care), Concurrent (during hospital stay), Retrospective (after care).
- **New York Specific**: Required benefits and mandated offers; New York HMO preventive-care rules; pure community rating. [NYDFS Individual and Small-Group Guidance](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/apps_and_licensing/health_insurers/guidance_ind_sg_nysoh)
- **Recall Cue**: HMO = Home-network Managed; PPO = Provider flexibility, Pay extra Out of network.
- **Tricky Question**: A member sees a specialist without a referral. The member has a PPO and the specialist is in-network. Which statement is most accurate?
  - A. The plan must deny the claim because all plans require PCP referrals.
  - B. The claim may be covered under the PPO's in-network terms.
  - C. The claim is paid only if the specialist is outside the network.
  - D. The visit is automatically paid as a fixed indemnity benefit.
  - *Answer*: **B**. PPO plans generally offer in-network access without requiring a PCP referral.
- **Detailed Note**: [[medical-plans-cost-sharing-managed-care]]

### Section 2: Accident & Health Basics (12% Weight)
- **Plain English & Example**: Protects against financial losses from injury, sickness, medical bills, or inability to work. A broken leg triggers medical benefits; a separate disability policy replaces lost wages.
- **Rules Tested**:
  - *Peril* is the cause of loss; *Loss* is the financial result.
  - *Comprehensive* is broad; *Limited* coverage only pays for named events.
  - *Hospital Indemnity* pays a stated amount per day (e.g. $300/day); does not equal hospital charges.
  - *Exclusions*: Workers' compensation, elective cosmetic surgery, experimental procedures.
  - *Medical Necessity*: Plan standard governs coverage; physician recommendation alone does not guarantee payment.
- **New York Specific**: State outline tests marketing, advertising, sales presentations, outlines of coverage, applications, delivery, replacement disclosures, and producer errors & omissions (E&O).
- **Recall Cue**: "Named event, named benefit" for limited policies.
- **Tricky Question**: Lee has a hospital-indemnity policy paying $300 per covered hospital day. The hospital charges $2,400 for a day. What does the policy generally pay?
  - A. The full $2,400
  - B. $300, if the policy's trigger and conditions are met
  - C. The bill minus the health plan deductible
  - D. Nothing unless Lee has no other insurance
  - *Answer*: **B**. A stated indemnity benefit is based on the policy schedule, not provider charges.
- **Detailed Note**: [[accident-and-health-insurance-basics-and-limited-policies]]

### Section 3: Individual Health Policy Provisions (10% Weight)
- **Plain English & Example**: Explains what the contract says, when coverage starts or stops, and what happens after a missed payment.
- **Rules Tested**:
  - *Entire Contract*: Policy + attached application + endorsements. Only executive officers can alter terms.
  - *Grace Periods*: 7 days (weekly), 10 days (monthly), 31 days (all other modes).
  - *Reinstatement*: Automatic on 45th day if not rejected. Accidents immediate; sickness after 10-day wait.
  - *Time Limit on Defenses (Incontestability)*: 2 years; misstatements contestable only within first 2 years (except fraud).
  - *Renewability*: Noncancelable (locks cancellation and rate); Guaranteed renewable (locks renewal, allows class-wide rate changes); Conditionally renewable; Optionally renewable; Cancelable.
  - *Coordination of Benefits (COB)*: Prevents double recovery when multiple plans apply.
- **New York Specific**: Read actual statutory wording; identify rights and duties; do not apply life rules to health.
- **Recall Cue**: "Noncancelable locks cancellation and rate; guaranteed renewable locks renewal."
- **Tricky Question**: An insurer may raise a guaranteed-renewable policy's premium for all policyholders in a permitted class but cannot refuse to renew an individual insured just because that person became ill. Which provision is this?
  - A. Noncancelable
  - B. Guaranteed renewable
  - C. Optionally renewable
  - D. Nonrenewable
  - *Answer*: **B**. Guaranteed renewable protects renewal rights but allows class premium changes.
- **Detailed Note**: [[individual-health-policy-provisions-and-renewability]]

### Section 4: Disability Income & Related Insurance (10% Weight)
- **Plain English & Example**: Replaces earnings if illness or injury prevents work. An eye surgeon who cuts her finger cannot operate, but could teach.
- **Rules Tested**:
  - *Own Occupation*: Unable to perform material duties of regular job (liberal).
  - *Any Occupation*: Unable to perform any occupation suited by education/training (strict).
  - *Elimination Period*: Waiting time before benefits begin (time deductible).
  - *Benefit Period*: Maximum duration benefits can continue.
  - *Presumptive Disability*: Automatic total disability for blindness, deafness, speech loss, or two limbs.
  - *COLA*: Increases benefits to match inflation; *Future Increase Option*: Allows purchasing coverage without evidence of insurability.
  - *Business Disability*: Business Overhead Expense (pays rent/utilities/staff salaries, NEVER owner salary; benefit taxable, expenses deductible); Key Person and Buy-Sell (premiums not deductible, benefits tax-free).
- **New York Specific: NY DBL**: Covers non-work-related disability only. Statutory benefit: **50% of average weekly wages, capped at $170/week, for up to 26 weeks, with a 7-day waiting period** (benefits start on day 8). Work injuries belong under Workers' Comp. [NY Workers' Compensation Board DBL](https://www.wcb.ny.gov/content/main/DisabilityBenefits/employee-disability-benefits.jsp) · [NYDFS DBL Checklist](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2023/07/dbl_checklist_20230703_form_fill.pdf)
- **Recall Cue**: Elimination = when benefits start; benefit period = how long they last.
- **Tricky Question**: A worker's covered non-work illness prevents work for 10 consecutive days. Under New York DBL's standard statutory terms, when can benefits first begin?
  - A. The first day
  - B. The eighth day
  - C. The tenth day
  - D. Only after 26 weeks
  - *Answer*: **B**. DBL has a 7-day waiting period; benefits begin on the 8th day.
- **Detailed Note**: [[disability-income-provisions-riders-business-uses]]

### Section 5: Federal Affordable Care Act (10% Weight)
- **Plain English & Example**: Sets national ground rules for individual and small-group health plans. Marketplace plans cannot reject applicants or charge more based on pre-existing conditions.
- **Rules Tested**:
  - *Pre-existing Conditions*: Mandatory coverage under federal law. [HealthCare.gov Pre-existing Conditions](https://www.healthcare.gov/coverage/pre-existing-conditions/)
  - *10 Essential Health Benefits (EHBs)*: Mandatory coverage categories; zero lifetime and annual dollar limits on EHBs. [HealthCare.gov Limits & EHBs](https://www.healthcare.gov/health-care-law-protections/lifetime-and-yearly-limits/)
  - *Actuarial Value & Metal Tiers*: Bronze 60%, Silver 70%, Gold 80%, Platinum 90%. Average population estimate, not individual bill guarantee.
  - *Subsidies*: Premium tax credits lower premiums; Cost-Sharing Reductions (CSR) lower out-of-pocket costs on Silver plans.
  - *Medical Loss Ratio (MLR)*: Minimum percentage of premium revenue spent on clinical care.
  - *Outdated Trap*: The federal individual mandate penalty was reduced to **$0** beginning in 2019!
- **New York Specific**: New York operates its own state-based marketplace, **NY State of Health**, and enforces pure community rating.
- **Recall Cue**: ACA: access, essential benefits, zero dollar caps, no pre-existing exclusions.
- **Tricky Question**: A Silver Marketplace plan has an actuarial value of about 70%. Does that mean it pays exactly 70% of every enrollee's bills?
  - A. Yes, every bill is divided 70/30.
  - B. No; it is an average estimate across a standard population.
  - C. Yes, but only for prescriptions.
  - D. No; actuarial value measures the insurer's profit.
  - *Answer*: **B**. Actuarial value is a standardized average population metric.
- **Detailed Note**: [[affordable-care-act-aca-and-essential-health-benefits]]

### Section 6: Group Health & Blanket Insurance (10% Weight)
- **Plain English & Example**: Issued to an employer or group holding the master policy; employees receive certificates. Blanket insurance covers shifting groups exposed to a common activity (e.g. passengers or sports teams) with no individual certificates.
- **Rules Tested**:
  - *Fully Insured*: Insurer bears claim risk.
  - *Self-Funded + ASO*: Employer retains claim risk and hires an administrator (Administrative Services Only); *Stop-Loss* protects employer against catastrophic claims (does not turn plan into insured plan for workers).
  - *COBRA*: Federal continuation (20+ employees, 18 mos termination, 36 mos divorce/death, 102% premium).
  - *Conversion*: Right to convert group health to individual policy within 31 days without medical exam.
  - *FSA vs. HSA*: FSA is employer-owned, use-it-or-lose-it; HSA requires qualifying High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP), individual ownership, portable.
- **New York Specific**: New York State Continuation ("Mini-COBRA") covers employers with **fewer than 20 workers and provides up to 36 months** of continuation!
- **Recall Cue**: "Group contract at the employer; certificate in the employee's hand."
- **Tricky Question**: An employer pays claims from its own funds and hires a company only to process claims. Which arrangement is this?
  - A. Fully insured plan
  - B. ASO for a self-funded plan
  - C. Medicare Supplement
  - D. Blanket coverage
  - *Answer*: **B**. The employer retains claims risk while an administrator performs services.
- **Detailed Note**: [[group-health-insurance-cobra-ny-continuation]]

### Section 7: Government Insurance Plans (9% Weight)
- **Plain English & Example**: Public programs covering specific risks or populations. Workers' Comp covers on-the-job injuries; Medicare covers seniors and disabled; Medicaid covers eligible low-income individuals.
- **Rules Tested**:
  - *Workers' Compensation*: Occupational injury/illness; 100% medical bills + wage replacement.
  - *Social Security Disability (SSDI)*: Severe total disability expected to last 12+ months or cause death; 5-month waiting period.
  - *Medicaid (Title XIX)*: Means-tested federal-state joint program; covers medical and custodial nursing care. [Medicaid.gov](https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid/eligibility-policy)
  - *Child Health Plus*: New York's child healthcare program.
  - *Medicare Structure*: Part A (Hospital/SNF), Part B (Outpatient/Medical), Part C (Medicare Advantage), Part D (Prescription Drugs). [Medicare.gov Parts of Medicare](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/medicare-basics/parts-of-medicare)
  - *CRITICAL RULE*: **Medicare DOES NOT cover ongoing custodial long-term care!** Check current Medicare costs online. [Medicare.gov Costs](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/costs/medicare-costs)
- **New York Specific: DBL**: Statutory maximum **$170/week**, up to **26 weeks**, **7-day waiting period**. Cash wage replacement only! [NY WCB DBL Overview](https://www.wcb.ny.gov/content/main/DisabilityBenefits/employee-disability-benefits.jsp)
- **Recall Cue**: A = Admission; B = Both doctors and outpatient; C = Choice of private plan; D = Drugs.
- **Tricky Question**: Which program is designed primarily for eligible people with limited income and is jointly funded by federal and state governments?
  - A. Medicare
  - B. Medicaid
  - C. Medigap
  - D. Medicare Advantage
  - *Answer*: **B**. Medicaid eligibility is state-administered within federal rules.
- **Detailed Note**: [[government-insurance-plans]]

### Section 8: Insurance Regulation & Producer Conduct (8% Weight)
- **Plain English & Example**: Producers help clients buy coverage, but must be licensed and truthful. Describing a limited indemnity policy as "full medical coverage" is illegal misrepresentation.
- **Rules Tested**:
  - *Agent* acts for insurer; *Broker* acts for insured.
  - *Misrepresentation*: False statements about policy terms or benefits.
  - *False Advertising*: Misleading promotional material.
  - *Rebating*: Offering unauthorized inducements to buy insurance.
  - *Defamation*: False malicious statements harmful to an insurer or producer.
  - *Unfair Discrimination*: Treating similar risks differently without actuarial justification.
  - *Fiduciary Duty*: Premiums held in trust; no commingling.
- **New York Specific**:
  - CE: **15 credit hours every 2 years**, including at least **3 hours in Ethics**.
  - Rebating: Promotional merchandise bearing producer's name allowed up to **$25 retail value**.
  - Reporting: **30 days** to report change of address or administrative/criminal actions.
  - Examination: Domestic insurers examined at least **once every 5 years**.
- **Recall Cue**: "Describe honestly, document clearly, handle money faithfully."
- **Tricky Question**: A producer tells a buyer that a specified-disease policy "covers all medical bills," although the policy pays only a stated amount for named conditions. What is the central problem?
  - A. Defamation
  - B. Misrepresentation
  - C. Coordination of benefits
  - D. Adverse selection
  - *Answer*: **B**. The producer misstated the policy's scope of coverage.
- **Detailed Note**: [[insurance-regulation-and-producer-conduct]]

### Section 9: General Insurance (6% Weight)
- **Plain English & Example**: Insurance transfers financial risk from the individual to an insurer.
- **Rules Tested**:
  - *Pure Risk*: Loss or no loss (insurable); *Speculative Risk*: Gain or loss (uninsurable).
  - *Peril* causes loss; *Hazard* increases the odds (Physical, Moral, Morale).
  - *Risk Handling (ARRTS)*: Avoidance, Reduction, Retention, Transfer, Sharing.
  - *Contract Characteristics*: Adhesion (drafted by insurer, ambiguities favor insured), Aleatory (unequal exchange), Unilateral (only insurer makes enforceable promise).
  - *Representation* (believed true) vs. *Warranty* (guaranteed true).
  - *Waiver* (giving up right) and *Estoppel* (prevented from reasserting right).
- **Recall Cue**: Peril produces loss; hazard raises the odds.
- **Tricky Question**: A person leaves a dangerous job to remove the chance of a work injury. Which risk-management method is this?
  - A. Retention
  - B. Avoidance
  - C. Sharing
  - D. Transfer
  - *Answer*: **B**. The person completely eliminated the exposure.
- **Detailed Note**: [[general-insurance-concepts-and-contract-law]]

### Section 10: Long-Term Care Insurance (5% Weight)
- **Plain English & Example**: Pays for assistance with daily living or supervision due to cognitive impairment. Covers care at home, adult day care, assisted living, or nursing homes.
- **Rules Tested**:
  - *Care Levels*: Skilled (licensed 24/7), Intermediate (intermittent medical), Custodial (non-medical daily assistance).
  - *Benefit Triggers*: Inability to perform at least **2 of 6 ADLs** (BATHED: Bathing, Ambulating/Transferring, Toileting, Continence, Eating, Dressing) or **Severe Cognitive Impairment**.
  - *Elimination & Benefit Periods*: Elimination = waiting period; Benefit period = duration of payout.
- **New York Specific: NYS Partnership for LTC**: Links qualified private LTC coverage to special Medicaid asset-protection benefits after policy benefits are exhausted. Features **Dollar-for-Dollar** and **Total Asset Protection** designs. [NYDFS LTC Glossary](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/consumers/health_insurance/long_term_care_insurance/glossary) · [NYDFS 2025 LTC Report](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/reports-and-publications/dfs-annual-reports/ltcrpt2025)
- **Recall Cue**: LTC trigger = ADLs or cognition; Medicare ≠ ongoing custodial care.
- **Tricky Question**: An insured needs help bathing and dressing but does not need skilled nursing. Which coverage is most directly designed for this ongoing assistance?
  - A. Medicare Part A only
  - B. Long-term care insurance, if the contract's benefit trigger is met
  - C. Workers' compensation
  - D. Hospital indemnity only
  - *Answer*: **B**. LTC insurance pays for custodial assistance when benefit triggers are met.
- **Detailed Note**: [[long-term-care-and-ny-partnership]]

### Section 11: Medicare Supplements & Senior Coverage (5% Weight)
- **Plain English & Example**: Medigap is private insurance that pays cost-sharing under Original Medicare. If you choose Medicare Advantage (Part C), you cannot buy Medigap to supplement it!
- **Rules Tested**:
  - *Medigap* works exclusively with Original Medicare (Parts A & B).
  - Standardized lettered plans (**Plans A through N**); Plan A is core basic benefit and mandatory offer.
  - Medicare Advantage (Part C) is an alternative private managed care plan.
- **New York Specific**: New York enforces **continuous year-round open enrollment and pure community rating** for Medigap! Mandatory **30-day free look**. [Medicare.gov Medigap Benefits](https://www.medicare.gov/health-drug-plans/medigap/basics/compare-plan-benefits)
- **Recall Cue**: Medigap fills gaps in Original Medicare; Advantage replaces that route.
- **Tricky Question**: A client enrolls in Medicare Advantage and asks you to sell a Medigap policy to pay that plan's copayments. What is the best response?
  - A. Medigap is designed to supplement Original Medicare, not Medicare Advantage.
  - B. Medigap automatically pays all Advantage copayments.
  - C. Medigap replaces Part D.
  - D. Medicare Advantage is the same thing as Medigap.
  - *Answer*: **A**. Medigap supplements Original Medicare only.
- **Detailed Note**: [[medicare-supplements-medigap-and-senior-coverage]]

## 4. Outdated Information & Traps to Avoid

1. **NYDFS Topic Locator**: The online locator is dated 2018–2019. Use it as a reference locator, not as the current outline. The **PSI Bulletin** governs exam scope and weights.
2. **Federal ACA Mandate Penalty**: Do NOT claim the individual mandate carries a federal tax penalty; **the federal penalty was reduced to $0 in 2019**.
3. **Medicare Costs**: Do not memorize old dollar deductibles or Part B premiums; Medicare costs change annually. Learn the coverage structure (60/90/150 days, 80/20 coinsurance).
4. **NY DBL vs. Workers' Comp**: Remember the **$170 weekly cap, 26 weeks, 7-day wait**. It applies to off-the-job injuries only and pays **$0 for medical bills**. Workers' Comp covers on-the-job injuries and pays 100% of medical bills.
5. **NYS Partnership LTC Minimums**: Daily benefit amounts can change; refer to current NYDFS reports rather than memorizing old static numbers.

## 5. One-Page Cram Sheet Summary
- **Exam**: 100 questions, 2 hours, 70% passing threshold.
- **Top Weight Focus**: Medical Plans (15%), A&H Basics (12%), Provisions (10%), Disability (10%), ACA (10%), Group (10%), Government (9%), Regulation (8%).
- **Cost Sharing**: Deductible first, copay fixed, coinsurance percentage, stop-loss caps out-of-pocket.
- **Health Provisions**: Grace periods = 7-10-31 days; Reinstatement = 10-day sickness wait; Claims = 20-15-90-60-3.
- **Renewability**: Noncan (locks cancel and rate); Guaranteed renewable (locks renewal, allows class rate change).
- **Disability**: Own Occ is narrower/easier for claimant than Any Occ; BOE covers office bills, NOT owner salary.
- **NY DBL**: Off-the-job, 50% AWW up to $170/wk, 26 weeks, 7-day wait, cash only.
- **COBRA / NY Continuation**: Federal = 20+ workers, 18 mos; NY Mini-COBRA = 1–19 workers, 36 mos.
- **ACA**: Pre-existing conditions covered; 10 EHBs; zero lifetime/annual dollar caps on EHBs; federal mandate penalty is $0; dependents to age 26 (NY Age 29 option).
- **Medicare & Senior**: Part A (hospital), B (medical 80/20), C (Advantage), D (drugs). Medigap supplements Original Medicare only. LTC requires 2 of 6 ADLs (BATHED) or cognitive trigger. NY Partnership protects assets from Medicaid.
- **Producer Rules**: 15-hr CE (3 hrs ethics); 30 days to report address change; $25 gift limit for rebating.

## 6. Fast Method for Scenario Questions ("Strike & Solve")
1. **Name the Coverage**: Medical expense, disability income, LTC, limited benefit, Medicare, Medicaid, or group continuation?
2. **Spot the Trigger**: Sickness, accident, disability definition, ADL/cognitive impairment, network status, or eligibility?
3. **Find the Controlling Feature**: Deductible, referral, elimination period, benefit period, renewal clause, coordination rule, or statutory program?
4. **Separate State from Federal**: Is the scenario asking about New York DBL, NY continuation (36 mos), NY Age 29, or state-specific LTC/Medigap rules?
5. **Reject Distractors that Promise Too Much**: Beware of "all bills," "any condition," "automatic payment," and "same as Medicare."
6. **Choose the Rule, Not What Seems Fair**: Base answers strictly on the contract terms or state statute.
