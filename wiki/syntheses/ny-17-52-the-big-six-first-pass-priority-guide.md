# NY 17-52: The Big Six First-Pass Priority Guide (67% Exam Weight)

## 1. Strategic Study Blueprint: The 67% First Pass

For the **New York Series 17-52 Accident & Health Exam**, the official outline assigns the largest weights to six core topics. Mastering these six areas accounts for **67% of the entire 100-question exam**:

```text

THE BIG SIX PRIORITY TOPICS (67% OF EXAM)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. Medical Plans                                  15%      CRITICAL TIER 1
2. Accident & Health Basics                       12%      CRITICAL TIER 1
3. Individual Health Policy Provisions            10%      HIGH TIER 2
4. Disability Income & Related Insurance          10%      HIGH TIER 2
5. Federal Affordable Care Act (ACA)              10%      HIGH TIER 2
6. Group Health & Blanket Insurance               10%      HIGH TIER 2

LTC (5%), Senior Coverage (5%).
```

## 2. The Big Six Topic Breakdown & Tested Traps

### 1. Medical Plans: Who You Can See and What You Pay (15% Weight)
- **Network Models**:
  - *HMO*: Usually lower cost; strictly use in-network doctors; primary care physician (PCP) gatekeeper coordinates care and writes referrals.
  - *PPO*: More provider choice; out-of-network care covered, but costs more; no gatekeeper or referrals needed.
  - *EPO*: Generally network-only except emergencies; usually no out-of-network benefits; no gatekeeper needed.
  - *POS*: Combines features; in-network care is cheaper; can go out-of-network at higher cost.
  - *Indemnity Plan*: Maximum provider freedom; plan reimburses covered medical expenses under its terms.
- **Cost Sharing Structure**:
  - *Deductible*: What you pay first out-of-pocket before benefits begin.
  - *Copay*: A set flat dollar amount per service (e.g., $25 per office visit).
  - *Coinsurance*: Your percentage share after the deductible (e.g., plan pays 80%, you pay 20%).
  - *Out-of-Pocket Maximum (Stop-Loss)*: The covered cost-sharing ceiling for the plan year; once reached, insurer pays 100%.
- **Mnemonic**: **D-C-C-M** = **D**eductible, **C**opay, **C**oinsurance, **M**aximum.
  - *Recall Cue*: Ask: *"What do I pay first, per visit, by percentage, and up to what cap?"*
- **Exam Trap**: *"Out of network"* does NOT always mean *"not covered."* It depends on the plan. HMO/EPO test tight network rules (out-of-network = $0 except emergency); PPO tests higher out-of-network cost-sharing.
- **Deep-Dive Note**: [[medical-plans-cost-sharing-managed-care]]

### 2. Policy Provisions: What the Contract Promises (10% Weight)
- **Contract Rules**:
  - *Entire Contract*: The policy and attached application make up the complete contract. Agents cannot change wording.
  - *Grace Period*: Extra time to pay late premiums before coverage lapses (7 days weekly, 10 days monthly, 31 days all others).
  - *Reinstatement*: Brings a lapsed policy back (accidents immediate; 10-day wait for sickness).
  - *Free Look (Right to Examine)*: Time to review and return a new policy for a 100% full refund under applicable rules.
- **Renewability Spectrum**:
  - *Noncancelable*: Insurer cannot cancel AND cannot raise premiums while paid, through the stated age/period.
  - *Guaranteed Renewable*: Insurer must renew, but may raise premiums for an entire rating class statewide.
  - *Conditionally Renewable*: Renewal can be refused only for stated contract conditions (e.g. reaching age 65).
  - *Cancellable*: Insurer can cancel at any time as the contract and law allow.
- **Mnemonic**: **N-G-C** = **N**oncancelable (no rate rise), **G**uaranteed renewable (group increase possible), **C**ancellable (can cancel).
- **Exam Trap**: Guaranteed renewable does **NOT** mean the premium can never rise!
- **Deep-Dive Note**: [[individual-health-policy-provisions-and-renewability]]

### 3. Disability Income: When the Policy Pays (10% Weight)
- Replaces lost income when illness or injury prevents working.
- **Core Parameters**:
  - *Elimination Period*: The waiting time before benefits start (time deductible).
  - *Benefit Period*: How long monthly benefits can continue.
  - *Own Occupation*: Unable to do the specific job or occupation specified by the policy (liberal).
  - *Any Occupation*: Unable to work in a qualifying job suited by education, training, or experience (strict).
  - *Residual / Partial Disability*: Able to work, but has a covered percentage loss of earnings or reduced duties.
  - *Presumptive Disability*: Severe specified losses (blindness, deafness, speech, two limbs) qualify without work-capacity tests.
- **Mnemonic**: **E then B** = **E**limination first; **B**enefits follow. Longer wait and shorter benefit period mean lower premiums.
- **Exam Trap**: Do not confuse *"unable to perform your job"* (Own Occ) with *"unable to perform any job"* (Any Occ). Read the exact definition in the question stem.
- **Deep-Dive Note**: [[disability-income-provisions-riders-business-uses]]

### 4. Group Coverage: Who Owns What? (10% Weight)
- **Structure**:
  - Employer or group sponsor holds the **Master Policy**.
  - Each covered worker receives a **Certificate of Insurance** summarizing their coverage.
- **Mechanics**:
  - *Conversion*: Right to convert group health to an individual policy within 31 days after group coverage terminates, without medical underwriting.
  - *Continuation (COBRA & NY Continuation)*: Temporarily keeps group coverage after qualifying events (102% premium).
  - *Coordination of Benefits (COB)*: Determines primary vs. secondary plan using the Birthday Rule to prevent double recovery.
- **Mnemonic**: **Master = Group; Certificate = Member**.
- **Exam Trap**: A certificate is NOT the master contract. For continuation, never assume coverage is free or automatic—the worker must elect it and pay up to 102% of the premium!
- **Deep-Dive Note**: [[group-health-insurance-cobra-ny-continuation]]

### 5. Federal ACA: What to Recall (10% Weight)
- **Key Tenets**:
  - Protections for pre-existing conditions (no denials or rate hikes).
  - Insurance Marketplaces (NY State of Health).
  - Premium Tax Credits (lower monthly premiums for eligible incomes).
  - Cost-Sharing Reductions (lower out-of-pocket costs on Silver plans).
  - 10 Essential Health Benefit categories (EHBs).
  - Ban on annual and lifetime dollar limits on essential benefits.
- **Mnemonic**: **P-M-T-E** = **P**re-existing conditions, **M**arketplace, **T**ax help, **E**ssential benefits.
- **Exam Trap**: Subsidies have strict eligibility rules; do not assume everyone qualifies. Distinguish premium help (tax credits) from out-of-pocket help (cost-sharing reductions).
- **Deep-Dive Note**: [[affordable-care-act-aca-and-essential-health-benefits]]

### 6. Long-Term Care & Senior Coverage (10% Combined Weight: 5% LTC + 5% Senior)
- **Long-Term Care (LTC)**: Covers qualifying long-term services (home care, adult day care, nursing facility).
  - *Benefit Triggers*: Inability to perform at least 2 of 6 ADLs, or severe cognitive impairment.
  - *The 6 ADLs*: Bathing, Dressing, Eating, Toileting, Transferring, Continence.
- **Senior Coverage Distinctions**:
  - *Medicare*: Health insurance for age 65+ and disabled; does NOT cover ongoing long-term custodial care!
  - *Medicaid*: Needs-based joint program that covers qualifying long-term nursing home care.
  - *Medigap*: Supplements Original Medicare (cannot be sold to supplement Medicare Advantage).
- **Mnemonic**: **Bath, Dress, Eat, Toilet, Transfer, Control** = Six ADLs.
- **Exam Trap**: Medicare and Medicaid are **NOT** interchangeable. Medigap and Medicare Advantage are completely separate, mutually exclusive paths.
- **Deep-Dive Notes**: [[long-term-care-and-ny-partnership]], [[medicare-supplements-medigap-and-senior-coverage]]

## 3. Plain-English Insurance Basics (Cause-Chance-Condition)

- **Risk**: The possibility of a loss occurring.
- **Peril**: The specific event that CAUSES the loss (sickness, accident, fire).
- **Hazard**: A condition that makes the loss more likely or severe (wet floor, smoking).
- **Mnemonic**: **Cause–Chance–Condition**:
  - **Peril causes** the loss.
  - **Risk is the chance** of loss.
  - **Hazard is the condition** that raises the chance.
- **Premium**: What you pay to keep coverage active.
- **Claim**: Formal request for policy benefits following a covered loss.
- **Insurable Interest**: A legitimate financial or family interest supporting insurance on someone's health or life (must exist at inception).
- **Indemnity**: Insurance restores a covered loss to the same financial position; it is not designed to create a profit!
- **Adverse Selection**: Tendency for higher-risk individuals to seek or continue insurance more aggressively than average risks.
- **Agent vs. Broker**:
  - *Agent*: Represents the **insurer** (company).
  - *Broker*: Represents the **insured** (customer).
- **Producer Conduct**:
  - *Misrepresentation*: Making false or misleading statements regarding terms or benefits.
  - *Rebating*: Offering improper financial inducements to buy insurance ($25 promotional gift cap in NY).
  - *Unfair Discrimination*: Treating similar risk classes differently without permitted actuarial justification.
