# NY Series 17-52 Accident & Health Five-Module Master Study Guide

## Executive Curriculum Overview
This master curriculum synthesizes the **New York Accident and Health Insurance Agent/Broker Examination (Series 17-52)** across the five mandatory domains of the GMetrix/PSI testing blueprint:
1. **Module 1**: Field Underwriting Procedures
2. **Module 2**: Other Insurance Concepts
3. **Module 3**: Policy Provisions, Clauses, and Riders
4. **Module 4**: Social Insurance
5. **Module 5**: Types of Policies

All legal citations are grounded in **New York Insurance Law (NYIL) Chapter 28**, **Title 11 of New York Codes, Rules and Regulations (11 NYCRR)**, the federal **Affordable Care Act (ACA)**, and **Title XVIII/XIX of the Social Security Act**.

# MODULE 1: FIELD UNDERWRITING PROCEDURES

## 1.1 The Producer's Role in Field Underwriting
- **Frontline Evaluation**: The producer (agent or broker) is the **field underwriter**. The producer personally interviews the applicant, inspects risks firsthand, and records truthful responses.
- **Soliciting and Completing the Application**:
  - The application is the **primary source** of underwriting information.
  - The producer must ensure every question is answered truthfully and completely.
  - **Alterations**: If an error is made on a paper application, the producer CANNOT white it out or scribble over it. The applicant must either correct the entry and **initial the change**, or complete a fresh application.
  - **Incomplete Applications**: If an application is submitted incomplete, the underwriter **must return it to the producer**. An incomplete application CANNOT be accepted with answers added later. If an insurer issues a policy on an incomplete application without requesting the missing facts, the insurer legally **waives** the right to that information.

## 1.2 Signatures, Representations & Insurable Interest
- **Required Signatures**: Both the **proposed insured** and the **producer** must sign the application. If the applicant is different from the insured (third-party ownership, e.g., parent on minor child or business on key executive), the **policyowner/applicant** must also sign.
- **Representations vs. Warranties (NYIL § 3105)**:
  - In New York, all statements made by an applicant on an accident and health application are deemed **representations, NOT warranties**.
  - **Representation**: A statement believed to be true to the best of the applicant’s knowledge.
  - **Material Misrepresentation**: A false statement that, had the insurer known the true facts, would have caused the insurer to reject the risk or charge a higher premium. In New York, a material misrepresentation voids coverage during the first 2 years.
  - **Warranty**: An absolute, literal promise of fact. Breach voids coverage automatically. Insurance applications in NY do **not** contain warranties.
- **Insurable Interest (NYIL § 3205)**:
  - Must exist **strictly at the time of application / inception**, NOT at the time of claim or loss.
  - Inherent in one's own health and life, or spouse/immediate family.
  - When a third party applies and names themselves beneficiary, insurable interest must be affirmatively established (e.g., economic loss upon disability).

## 1.3 Sources of Underwriting Information
- **Attending Physician's Statement (APS)**: Ordered only when the application or screening reveals specific questionable medical concerns requiring clinical records from the treating physician.
- **Medical Information Bureau (MIB)**: Centralized trade database containing confidential coded medical impairments. **Never contains credit reports, moral character interviews, or physical exam reports.** An insurer cannot reject an applicant based solely on an MIB code without independent medical verification.
- **Inspection Report (Investigative Consumer Report)**: Evaluates moral character, financial standing, habits, and lifestyle through interviews. Governed by the **Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA)**. Notice of information practices must be given within 3 days.

## 1.4 Premium Receipts & Policy Effective Dates
```
[Application + Premium Submitted] 
       |
       +--> Conditional Receipt Issued -> Coverage retroactive to date of application/exam IF approved as applied.
       |
[Application Submitted WITHOUT Premium]
       |
       +--> No Receipt Issued -> Insurer makes offer -> Producer delivers policy -> 
            Collects full premium + Signed Statement of Continued Good Health.
```
- **Conditional Receipt**: Provides coverage effective either on the date of application or the date of the medical exam (whichever is later), provided the applicant was insurable exactly as applied for under standard company guidelines.
- **Binding Receipt (Unconditional / Temporary)**: Binds immediate coverage for a specified timeframe (e.g., 30–60 days) regardless of insurability. (Rare in health; common in property/casualty).
- **Delivery Protocol**:
  - **Prepaid application**: Producer reviews policy, exclusions, and riders. Free Look countdown begins on **physical delivery date**. No Statement of Good Health needed.
  - **Non-prepaid application**: Producer must collect full initial premium AND obtain a signed **Statement of Continued Good Health** verifying no changes in health occurred.

## 1.5 Replacement & Regulatory Disclosures
- **New York Insurance Regulation 60 (11 NYCRR Part 51)**:
  - Strictly regulates replacement of life insurance and annuities; sets gold standard for disclosures.
  - When replacing health insurance, the producer must provide a **Notice Regarding Replacement of Accident and Sickness Insurance** warning of new pre-existing condition exclusions and probationary periods.
  - **Free-Look Period**: Standard individual health policies require 10 to 20 days; Medicare Supplement, Long-Term Care, and Replacement policies in NY mandate a **30-day Free Look** with 100% full premium refund.

# MODULE 2: OTHER INSURANCE CONCEPTS

## 2.1 Risk, Peril, Hazard & Indemnity
- **Pure Risk vs. Speculative Risk**: Only **pure risk** (chance of loss only, no chance of gain) is insurable. Speculative risk (gambling, stock trading) is uninsurable.
- **Peril**: The direct cause of loss (e.g., accidental fall, sickness, cancer).
- **Hazard**: A condition that creates or increases the probability of a peril occurring:
  - **Physical Hazard**: Slippery ice, defective wiring, blind spot in eye.
  - **Moral Hazard**: Dishonesty, criminal intent, filing fraudulent claims.
  - **Morale Hazard**: Carelessness, indifference ("Who cares if I leave the stove on, insurance pays").
- **Indemnity Doctrine**: Insurance is designed to restore the insured to the same financial position they enjoyed prior to the loss, **without profiting**.

## 2.2 Cost Sharing Mechanisms
- **Deductible**: Dollar amount the insured must pay out-of-pocket before the insurer begins paying benefits. Can be per-cause or annual calendar-year.
- **Coinsurance**: Percentage cost split after the deductible (e.g., 80/20 where insurer pays 80%, insured pays 20%).
- **Copayment**: Flat dollar amount paid per service (e.g., $25 per office visit, $15 per generic prescription). Does not usually count toward deductible.
- **Out-of-Pocket Maximum (Stop-Loss)**: The maximum cumulative amount the insured will pay in deductibles and coinsurance in a calendar year. Once reached, the insurer pays **100% of covered expenses** for the remainder of that year.

## 2.3 Coordination of Benefits (COB) & The Birthday Rule
- Prevents overinsurance and profiting from duplicate group coverage (limits total reimbursement to 100% of allowable expenses).
- **Primary vs. Secondary**:
  - Employee's own employer plan is **Primary**; spouse's plan is **Secondary**.
  - **Dependent Children (The Birthday Rule)**: The plan of the parent whose birthday (month and day only, NOT year) falls **earlier in the calendar year** is primary. If both parents have the same birthday, the plan in effect longest is primary. (In divorce/separation, court custody decree governs).

## 2.4 Subrogation & Pre-Existing Conditions
- **Subrogation**: Legal transfer of the insured’s right to recover damages from a negligent third party to the insurance company after paying the claim. Prevents double recovery.
- **Pre-Existing Conditions under the ACA**:
  - The Affordable Care Act strictly **prohibits pre-existing condition exclusions** on individual and group comprehensive major medical plans.
  - Pre-existing exclusions remain legal only in **excepted benefit / limited policies** (e.g., indemnity, critical illness, disability income, short-term health).

## 2.5 Taxation of Premiums and Benefits
| Policy Type | Premium Tax Status | Benefit Tax Status | Statutory Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Individual Medical Expense** | Deductible only if medical expenses exceed **7.5% of AGI** (itemized). | **100% Tax-Free** | Benefits reimburse medical losses. |
| **Individual Disability Income** | **Not deductible** (paid with after-tax dollars). | **100% Income Tax-Free** | Funded with post-tax dollars. |
| **Group Disability (Employer Paid)** | Employer deducts premiums as business expense. | **100% Taxable Income** to disabled employee. | Benefit follows premium tax deductibility. |
| **Business Overhead Expense (BOE)** | Premiums are **tax-deductible** business expenses. | Benefits are **taxable**, but offset when spent on deductible rent/salaries. | Keeps business operational. |
| **Key Person Disability** | Premiums are **NOT deductible**. | Benefits are **100% Tax-Free** to business. | Protects corporate balance sheet. |

## 3.2 The 11 Optional Provisions (NYIL § 3216)
1. **Change of Occupation**: If insured changes to a more hazardous job, benefits are **reduced** to what premium would have bought. If less hazardous, premium is reduced and excess refunded.
2. **Misstatement of Age**: Insurer adjusts benefit to what premium would have purchased at correct age. Policy is **never voided**.
3. **Other Insurance in This Insurer**: Limits total coverage with one insurer to avoid overinsurance; excess premiums refunded.
4. **Insurance with Other Insurers**: Pro-rates benefits if duplicate coverage without notice.
5. **Relation of Earnings to Insurance (Average Earnings)**: Prevents disability benefits from exceeding monthly earnings over prior 2 years. Minimum benefit floor is $200/mo.
6. **Unpaid Premium**: Deducted from claim payment.
7. **Cancellation**: Insurer must give **5 days' written notice** in standard model (NY restricts cancelable policies).
8. **Conformity with State Statutes**: Automatically amends policy to meet minimum state law.
9. **Illegal Occupation**: Insurer not liable for losses during commission of a felony.
10. **Intoxicants and Narcotics**: Insurer not liable for loss sustained while intoxicated or under influence of non-prescribed narcotics.

## 3.3 Renewability Spectrum
```
[Noncancelable]       -> Cannot cancel + Rates GUARANTEED never to increase. (Most expensive)
[Guaranteed Renew]    -> Cannot cancel + Rates CAN increase by whole class only.
[Conditionally Renew] -> Insurer can decline renewal ONLY for stated conditions in contract.
[Optionally Renew]    -> Insurer can decline renewal on any policy anniversary.
[Cancelable]          -> Insurer can cancel anytime with written notice (unearned premium refunded).
```

# MODULE 4: SOCIAL INSURANCE

## 4.1 Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI)
- **Eligibility**: Fully insured status (40 credits / 10 years of covered work) and disability insured status (20 credits in last 40 quarters).
- **Definition of Disability**: Inability to engage in any **Substantial Gainful Activity (SGA)** due to a medically determinable impairment expected to last at least **12 continuous months or result in death**. (Strictest definition in law).
- **Waiting Period**: **5 full calendar months**. Benefits begin in the 6th month.

## 4.2 Medicare Parts A, B, C, and D
- **Eligibility**: Age 65+, individuals of any age on SSDI for **24 months**, or persons with End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) or ALS.
- **Part A (Hospital Insurance)**: Funded by FICA payroll taxes. Premium-free for those with 40 quarters. Covers inpatient hospital (up to 90 days per benefit period + 60 lifetime reserve days), skilled nursing facility (up to 100 days; days 1–20 100% paid, days 21–100 daily copay), home health, and hospice.
- **Part B (Medical Insurance)**: Optional, funded by monthly premiums ($174.70/mo baseline in recent data) + general federal revenues. Covers physician fees, outpatient services, ambulatory surgery, lab tests, and durable medical equipment. Standard 80/20 coinsurance after annual Part B deductible.
- **Part C (Medicare Advantage)**: Private managed care plans (HMO/PPO) replacing Original Medicare (Parts A & B). Often includes Part D prescription coverage. Cannot be paired with MediGap!
- **Part D (Prescription Drug)**: Private standalone prescription drug plans. Standard benefit includes deductible, initial coverage limit, coverage gap ("donut hole" closed under ACA to 25% cost-share), and catastrophic coverage.

## 4.3 Medicare Supplement (MediGap) vs. Medicare Advantage
- **MediGap**: Private insurance designed to pay the deductibles, coinsurance, and copayments of **Original Medicare (Parts A & B)**. Standardized Plans A through N.
- **New York Law**: New York requires **continuous year-round open enrollment** and **pure community rating** for all MediGap policies (insurers cannot reject or charge higher rates based on age, health, or claims history).
- **Strict Rule**: A producer CANNOT legally sell a MediGap policy to someone enrolled in Medicare Advantage.

## 4.4 Medicaid (Title XIX)
- State-federal program providing health coverage for low-income and medically indigent individuals.
- Covers custodial nursing home care (which Medicare excludes).
- **Medicaid Look-Back Period**: **60 months (5 years)** on asset transfers to determine nursing home eligibility.

## 4.5 Workers' Comp vs. New York Disability Benefits Law (NY DBL)
| Feature | Workers' Compensation | New York Disability Benefits Law (NY DBL) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Scope of Coverage** | **On-the-job (Occupational)** accidents and illness. | **Off-the-job (Non-Occupational)** sickness and injury. |
| **Medical Expenses** | **Unlimited 100% medical care** covered. | **NO medical benefits** (cash wage replacement only). |
| **Wage Benefit** | 66 2/3% of Average Weekly Wage (AWW). | **50% of AWW up to statutory cap ($170/week)**. |
| **Maximum Duration** | Permanent or temporary as determined by board. | **26 weeks max** in any 52-consecutive-week period. |
| **Waiting Period** | 7 days (retroactive if disabled > 14 days). | **7 days waiting period** (benefits begin on day 8). |

## 4.6 Long-Term Care (LTC) Financing & The NYS Partnership
- **Medicare Exclusion**: Medicare **DOES NOT PAY FOR CUSTODIAL CARE**. It covers up to 100 days of skilled nursing only.
- **LTC Benefit Triggers**: Inability to perform at least **2 of 6 ADLs (BATHED)** for 90+ days, or **Severe Cognitive Impairment**.
- **Respite Care**: Pays for substitute caregivers to provide temporary relief to unpaid family caregivers.
- **New York State Partnership for Long-Term Care**: Purchase of an approved NY Partnership policy allows the insured to exhaust policy benefits and then qualify for New York Medicaid while retaining **100% of their personal assets (Total Asset Protection)** without spend-down!

# MODULE 5: TYPES OF POLICIES

## 5.1 Fee-For-Service vs. Managed Care Plans
```
                Network Freedom vs. Cost Control
[Fee-For-Service] -> Any doctor, bills submitted, highest cost.
[HMO]             -> Closed network, PCP Gatekeeper, Capitation, No out-of-network coverage (except emergency).
[POS]             -> HMO base with out-of-network option (higher coinsurance/deductible).
[EPO]             -> In-network only, NO PCP gatekeeper required.
[PPO]             -> In-network discounted fee-for-service, Out-of-network covered at lower rate, NO gatekeeper.
```
- **HMO (Health Maintenance Organization)**: Focuses on preventive care; uses primary care physician (PCP) gatekeepers to control specialist referrals; providers paid via fixed monthly **capitation**.
- **PPO (Preferred Provider Organization)**: Contracted network offering discounted fee-for-service rates. No gatekeeper. In-network paid at 80-90%, out-of-network paid at 60-70%.
- **EPO (Exclusive Provider Organization)**: Hybrid requiring members to stay in-network for non-emergency care, but does **not** require a gatekeeper referral to see specialists.
- **POS (Point-of-Service)**: "Open HMO" allowing out-of-network visits if the member is willing to pay higher deductibles and coinsurance.

## 5.2 Disability Income Insurance
- **Benefit Trigger**:
  - **Own Occupation ("Own Occ")**: Insured is unable to perform the material and substantial duties of their **own specific occupation**. Easiest to qualify; most expensive.
  - **Any Occupation ("Any Occ")**: Insured is unable to perform the duties of **any occupation** for which they are reasonably suited by education, training, or experience. Stricter; less expensive.
  - **Presumptive Disability**: Total loss of sight, speech, hearing, or use of any two limbs automatically qualifies the insured for full total disability benefits **regardless of ability to work**, waiving elimination periods.
- **Elimination Period**: Time between disability onset and benefit payment start (acts as a deductible in time, e.g., 30, 60, 90, 180 days). Longer elimination period = lower premium.
- **Benefit Period**: Maximum duration benefits are paid (e.g., 2 years, 5 years, to age 65, lifetime).

## 5.3 Limited & Supplemental Health Policies
- **Hospital Indemnity / Confinement**: Pays a **flat daily, weekly, or monthly dollar amount** (e.g., $200/day) directly to the insured during hospital confinement, regardless of actual medical bills.
- **Specified Disease (Critical Illness)**: Pays a lump-sum cash benefit upon diagnosis of a covered illness (e.g., stroke, invasive cancer, heart attack).
- **Accident-Only**: Covers medical expenses or death/dismemberment resulting solely from accidental bodily injury; excludes all sickness.
- **Dental & Vision**: Basic and major diagnostic, preventive, restorative, and orthodontic services.

## High-Yield Mnemonic Index
- **ACCL**: 4 elements of a contract (**A**greement, **C**onsideration, **C**ompetent parties, **L**egal purpose).
- **BATHED**: 6 Activities of Daily Living (**B**athing, **A**mbulating, **T**oileting, **H**ygienic continence, **E**ating, **D**ressing).
