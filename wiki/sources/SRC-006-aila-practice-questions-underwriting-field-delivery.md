## 3. Key Findings, Rules & Tested Points

### A. Underwriting & Field Underwriting
- **Primary Source**: The **Application** is the primary foundation for all underwriting decisions. Inspection reports, MIB, and medical exams provide supplemental confirmation.
- **Incomplete Applications**: An incomplete application **CANNOT** be accepted with information added later. It must be returned to the producer for completion. If an insurer issues a policy with unanswered questions, the insurer legally waives the right to that information.
- **Underwriting Sequence**: The underwriting evaluation process begins immediately once the application is submitted and initial premium is paid.
- **Medical Information Bureau (MIB)**: A non-profit trade association database storing coded medical impairment information. It does **not** contain credit history, moral inspection reports, or physician examination files.
- **Attending Physician's Statement (APS)**: Ordered when the underwriter needs clinical depth on questionable medical conditions disclosed in the medical history.
- **Inspection Report (Consumer Investigation Report)**: Investigates moral character, financial standing, habits, and lifestyle through interviews with associates. Governed strictly by the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA).

### B. Premium Calculation & Actuarial Tables
- **Gross Premium Formula**: $\text{Gross Premium} = \text{Risk (Mortality/Morbidity)} + \text{Expenses (Loading)} - \text{Interest}$.
- **Net Premium Formula**: $\text{Net Premium} = \text{Risk} - \text{Interest}$.
- **Actuarial Tables**:
  - **Morbidity Tables**: Used in health insurance to predict illness, disability, and accident frequency.
  - **Mortality Tables**: Used in life insurance to predict probability of death by age and gender.

### C. Risk Classifications
- **Preferred Risk**: Healthier than average, lowest mortality/morbidity risk, receives **discounted premiums**.
- **Standard Risk**: Average risk profile, standard rate.
- **Substandard (Rated) Risk**: Below-average health or hazardous lifestyle; policy is issued as a **rated policy** with **higher premiums** (flat extra, rated-up age, or percentage table rating).
- **Declined**: Risk exceeds underwriting guidelines; rejected.

### D. Policy Delivery Rules
- **Delivery with Prepaid Application**: Coverage is already effective (subject to conditional receipt). Producer's duty is to **review policy benefits, riders, and exclusions** with the client. No additional premium or Statement of Continued Good Health is collected.
- **Free-Look Trigger**: The 10-day (or 30-day replacement) Free Look countdown starts on the **date of physical delivery** to the policyowner, NOT on the policy effective date or application date.

### E. Life Policy Mechanics & Occupation Changes
- **Change of Occupation**: In life insurance, policies remain **in effect as issued** at original rates even if the insured takes up a hazardous occupation (e.g., librarian becoming a rodeo bull rider). Contrast with Disability Income, where benefits are reduced or premiums increased upon hazardous occupation change.
- **Convertible Term**: Allows converting term insurance to permanent insurance (e.g., whole life) **WITHOUT evidence of insurability**.
- **Viatical Settlements**: The sale of a policy by a terminally/chronically ill person (viator) to a third party. Payout is exempt from federal income tax under IRC § 101(g) only if life expectancy is **less than 24 months (2 years)**, NOT 5 years! Free look is mandatory. Viatical company becomes owner and beneficiary.

### F. Health & Long-Term Care Essentials
- **Long-Term Care (LTC)**: Primary peril covered is **aging** (chronic inability to perform ADLs), not acute accidents or death.
- **Respite Care**: LTC benefit designed to provide temporary relief to primary family caregivers.
- **Ambulatory Care**: Outpatient medical care not requiring overnight hospital admission.
- **Medical Expense Exclusions**: Covers medical treatments, prescription drugs, HIV-AIDS, and accidental injuries. **Pain and suffering** is an excluded tort/liability damage.

## 4. New York State Specific Mandates
- **Free-Look Period**: Standard policies require 10 to 30 days; NY Insurance Regulation 60 mandates **30 days** on all replacement policies.
- **Representations vs. Warranties**: Under NYIL § 3105, all statements on applications are representations. Incomplete applications cannot be modified without applicant sign-off.
- **Viatical and Life Settlements**: Regulated in New York under Article 78 of the Insurance Law, requiring provider licensing, consumer disclosures, and mandatory 15-day rescission/cooling-off periods.
